Read from the record · Ohio 24-508-EL-ATA · Georgia 44280

Approved as modified: the language you have to file exists in no filing

Two commissions, two dockets, one shape. The text a company must put in a compliance tariff is not in the filing and not in the order. It is the difference between them, and nobody writes it down.

On 9 July 2025 the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio closed a fourteen-month case with this ordering paragraph.

ORDERED, That the 10/23 Stipulation be approved and adopted, subject to
modifications consistent with this Order.
¶ 171, Opinion and Order, 9 July 2025 · oh-24-508-EL-ATA-opinion-and-order.txt · commission's copy

That is all of it. The ordering section runs from ¶ 170 to ¶ 176 and not one of those seven paragraphs says what the modifications are. AEP Ohio has to file a tariff. The text of that tariff is the stipulation plus something, and the something is somewhere in the ninety-five pages in front.

It is in ¶ 126.

the Commission modifies the 10/23 Stipulation’s collateral
provisions.  We find that, the collateral requirements must either be met by the data center
customer or the customer’s financial sponsor, so long as the sponsor is a co-signer on the
contract with AEP Ohio.
¶ 126, same order · oh-24-508-EL-ATA-opinion-and-order.txt · commission's copy

Now look for that in the stipulation. Its Exhibit A sets out the Collateral Requirement and gives three ways to satisfy it: a parent or affiliate guarantee, a letter of credit, or cash. Here is the first.

1. A guarantee from the ultimate parent or a corporate affiliate of the customer for the full Collateral Requirement,
so long as the guarantor has both (a) a credit rating of at least A- from S&P and A3 from Moody’s and (b)
Liquidity greater than ten times the Collateral Requirement; or
Joint Stipulation and Recommendation, 23 October 2024, Exhibit A, Schedule DCT · oh-24-508-EL-ATA-stipulation-2024-10-23-joint-ex-1.txt lines 1232–1234 · commission's copy

No sponsor. No co-signer. I counted both terms across both documents: co-signer appears once in the ninety-nine pages of the order and never in the thirty-one pages of the stipulation it modifies. Financial sponsor, the same — once, and never. So the operative words of the compliance tariff, the ones that will bind a data centre customer and its parent, sit in neither document. They are the difference between the two.

This is not a reading problem I invented

The Ohio Blockchain Council filed for rehearing and asked the Commission what it had meant.

requests
clarification on the meaning of “financial sponsor.”
¶ 53, Entry on Rehearing, 3 September 2025 · oh-24-508-EL-ATA-entry-on-rehearing.txt · commission's copy

Fifty-six days after the order, the Commission answered.

we agree with the Company that the term “financial
sponsor” is a well-known legal term of art that does not require further clarification, we will
provide additional context for OBC, as requested.
¶ 55, Entry on Rehearing · oh-24-508-EL-ATA-entry-on-rehearing.txt · commission's copy

Then it supplied a definition that had appeared in neither document.

As contemplated in the Order, “financial
sponsor” is meant to include any entity that co-signs a LOA or ESA alongside a data center
customer and serves to satisfy the data center customer’s creditworthiness criteria and/or
posts either all, or a portion of, the collateral requirement for the data center customer.
¶ 55, Entry on Rehearing · oh-24-508-EL-ATA-entry-on-rehearing.txt · commission's copy

The term needed no clarification, and here is the clarification. Whatever you make of that, the practical point stands: anyone drafting the compliance tariff between 9 July and 3 September was drafting against a term with no definition on the record, and the parties to the case could not agree what it meant.

Georgia 44280 fails the same reconciliation somewhere else

On 28 January 2025 the Georgia Public Service Commission approved Georgia Power's large-load rules with five requirements attached. The fourth was the compliance filing.

The Company shall make a compliance filing of the relevant tariffs for applicable customers to reflect the changes to the rules and regulations.
Order on Georgia Power Company's Revision to Rules and Regulations Tariff Compliance Filing, 17 April 2025, reciting the 28 January order · ga-44280-order-rules-regs-tariff-compliance.txt line 43 · commission's copy

The company filed on 28 February: ten tariffs, twenty-five sheets, a clean copy and a tracked-changes copy in one ZIP on the commission's server. The tracked copy is the one you would open, because it is the company telling you what it changed.

Extract both to text and diff them word by word and you get eighty-seven changed regions. Eighty-two are the effective date, the revision number, or a heading that the extractor placed somewhere else on the page. Line 9 of each file, the pair that carries a real edit:

With Bills Rendered for the Billing Month of April, 2025
clean · ga-44280-rules-regs-tariff-compliance-clean.txt line 9 
With Bills Rendered for the Billing Month of JanuaryApril, 2025
tracked · ga-44280-rules-regs-tariff-compliance-tracked.txt line 9 · both from the same ZIP

January struck, April inserted, fused by the extractor because struck and inserted text sit next to each other on the page. Ugly, but legible, and a real change reported.

Now the change the filing exists to make. Lines 25 to 26 of the clean copy:

This  tariff  is  not
applicable  to  customers  with  an  expected  total  peak  demand  of  100  MW  or  greater  at  one  or  more
premises located on one tract or contiguous tracts of land.
Schedule OGS-22, page 1 of 2, APPLICABILITY · ga-44280-rules-regs-tariff-compliance-clean.txt lines 25–26 · commission's copy

Lines 26 to 27 of the tracked copy are those characters exactly. Not similar — identical, double spaces and all. The clause appears eleven times in each file, and not one of the eighty-seven changed regions touches the string 100 or the string MW.

That generalises, and it is worth holding on to. The tracked copy reports the month the tariff takes effect and stays silent about the sentence that made it a new revision. All twenty-five sheets go from revision Original to revision First. The clause is why.

The Georgia order does not agree with itself

The 17 April order adopts Staff's recommendations wholesale — WHEREFORE IT IS ORDERED, that Staff’s recommendations … are hereby adopted — and then restates them as ordering clauses. The restatement is not the same text.

… will apply this framework.
Staff recommendation 3, FINDINGS OF FACT · ga-44280-order-rules-regs-tariff-compliance.txt line 67 
… will apply the framework in the same manner as provided and demonstrated to Staff.
ORDERED FURTHER · same file, line 98 · commission's copy

Both sentences are in one order, four pages apart. The second adds a compliance standard the first does not have. Which one binds the contract language? Somebody has to choose, and the order does not help them.

What you can do on Monday, with nothing you have to buy

Read the order backwards

Start at the ordering section, because that is what binds, then search the body for modif, we direct, we find that. In Ohio that is one hit out of 176 paragraphs. The ordering paragraphs will not point you to it.

Keep a three-column sheet before you touch tariff text

Filed language, the paragraph that changes it, resulting language. One row per provision. The third column is the one nobody has written yet, and writing it is the job. Carry the paragraph number next to every line you draft, so that when staff asks why, you answer in one move.

Never diff a clean copy against a redline

Diff clean against the previous clean. A redline shows you replacements and hides insertions, which is the wrong half. Open the redline with your eyes for the insertions; it is a document for reading, not for comparing.

Ask early, in writing

Where an order's operative clause differs from the recommendation it says it adopts, or turns on a term that appears nowhere else, file for clarification before the effective date. In Ohio somebody did, and it took 56 days to come back. That is a schedule you can plan around only if you start it on day one.

How the numbers were made

Every count here came from Python's difflib.SequenceMatcher over whitespace-normalised word lists, run against the extracted text of the filings named above, each of which carries a provenance record with its source URL and a SHA-256 that recomputes. The Georgia figure is the count of non-equal opcodes between the clean and tracked word lists, then split by whether a region's tokens are drawn only from the effective-date, revision and heading vocabulary. The Ohio paragraph positions come from splitting on the pilcrow marks the Commission prints. Term counts are plain regular expressions, case-insensitive. Open the files and repeat it; if a number here is wrong I would rather hear it than not.

A disclosure, not a pitch. I build Strata, which reads successive versions of a proceeding and refuses to assert a claim whose citation does not verify. This article exists because the Ohio and Georgia dockets beat the first version of that check, and I would rather publish the failure than the feature. Nothing above needs software.
