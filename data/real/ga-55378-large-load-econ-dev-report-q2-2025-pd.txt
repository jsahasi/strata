Introduction 

Georgia Power Company (“Georgia Power” or the “Company”) has a responsibility to serve the needs 
of its customers — today and for generations to come. Our long-term integrated resource planning 
processes, along with the Georgia Public Service Commission’s (“Commission”) triennial Integrated 
Resource Plan proceedings, serve as the foundation of our ability to provide clean, safe, reliable, and 
a0ordable energy to Georgians and to serve the state’s growing economy. Georgia is the number one 
state to do business due in large part to the e0ectiveness of this planning process that ensures we 
are able to meet energy demands today and in the future.  

Since the Company’s last large load quarterly report in May, Georgia’s economy continues to prosper. 
And as the state’s economic prospects remain bright, the trends leading up to the 2023 Integrated 
Resource Plan (“IRP”) Update ﬁling and as presented in the 2025 IRP remain consistent, having now 
continued for more than two years. 

The latest data continue to support Georgia Power’s expectation for continued and robust economic 
growth  in  Georgia  and  the  timing  of  new  large  load  customers.  As  illustrated  in  this  report,  the 
following growth trends continue: 

  The pipeline of potential economic development projects continues to grow.  

o  As of June 30, 2025, the total pipeline of economic development projects through the 
mid-2030s has increased by 4,400 MW to 59,000 MW. Of this, 56,800 MW represent 
large load economic development projects. 

o  For  the  near-term  winter  of  2028/2029,  the  large  load  economic  development 

pipeline has increased by 600 MW to 25,800 MW. 

  Large load customers continue to materialize and progress with construction. 

o 

In the near-term, projects that have broken ground represent 3,265 MW of the total 
6,261 MW of customer commitments for the winter of 2028/2029. 

o  Table 1 in this report provides a summary of the construction status of the 26 large 
load projects that have executed a Request for Electric Service (“RFS”) as of June 30, 
2025.  Thirteen  of  these  projects  have  broken  ground,  and  thirteen  are  pending 
construction.

Changes in the Economic Development Pipeline 

Since the ﬁrst quarter 2025 report, as of June 30, 2025, the total pipeline of economic development 
projects through the mid-2030s has increased from 54,600 MW to 59,000 MW – an increase of 4,400 
MW. Of the 59,000 MW in the total pipeline, 56,800 MW represent large load economic development 
projects.1  The  large  load  projects  have  increased  by  a  net  4,700  MW,  resulting  from  the  following 
changes since the last report: 

  6,000 MW that entered the pipeline;  
  A net increase of 400 MW for projects already in the pipeline that increased their projected 

load; and 

  1,600 MW that exited the pipeline. 

Since  our  last  report,  in  the  near-term  (winter  of  2028/2029),  the  total  pipeline  of  projects  has 
increased  by  1,200  MW  to  28,900  MW,  while  the  large  load  economic  development  pipeline  has 
increased by 600 MW to 25,800 MW. Further details about the existing, new, and former projects in 
the pipeline are provided in the attachment to this report. 

Changes in Commitments from Large Load Customers 

As  the  total  and  large  load  economic  development  pipelines  have  grown  and  strengthened,  the 
number of commitments to Georgia Power for service from large load customers has remained the 
same  this  quarter.  One  committed  project  has  requested  a  change  in  load  causing  near-term 
commitments to increase 615 MW to 6,261 MW and total committed load to increase 525 MW to 
8,799 MW, representing 26 committed large load projects. 

Table 1 below provides a summary of the construction status of the 26 committed large load projects, 
all of which have executed an RFS with Georgia Power. Thirteen of these projects have broken ground, 
and  thirteen  are  pending  construction.  This  evidence  clearly  indicates  that  these  large  load 
customers are materializing and making progress without material delays. In the near-term, projects 
that have broken ground represent 3,265 MW of the total 6,261 MW of customer commitments for 
the winter of 2028/2029.

Conclusion 
Since March 2025:  

  The total economic development pipeline has expanded from approximately 54,600 MW to 
approximately 59,000 MW through the mid-2030s, representing an increase of 4,400 MW; 

  Near-term 2028/2029 large loads have increased by 600 MW; and 
  Customer  commitments increased  to  8,799  MW  and  near-term  commitments have grown 

615 MW to 6,261 MW 

Since the ﬁling of the 2023 IRP Update in October 2023: 

  The total economic development pipeline has expanded from approximately 17,000 MW to 
approximately 59,000 MW through the mid-2030s, representing an increase of 42,000 MW; 

  Near-term 2028/2029 large loads have increased by approximately 15,300 MW; and 
  Customer  commitments  have  expanded  from  3,612  MW  to  8,799  MW,  representing  an 

increase of 5,187 MW. 

This information shows that Georgia Power’s load forecast is materializing and that the constructive 
outcomes of the 2023 IRP Update and 2025 IRP support continued economic growth in Georgia. 

The Company will continue to monitor economic growth and will keep the Commission apprised of 
large load activity consistent with the requirements of the Commission’s Order Adopting Stipulated 
Agreement in the 2023 IRP Update and Order Adopting Stipulation in the 2025 IRP.
