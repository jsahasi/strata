Three-Part Test for Consideration of First Stipulation 
19 
Q. 
What is the three-part test? 
20 
A. 
I understand that a stipulation could be approved or approved with modifications if it: 
21 
1) is the product of serious bargaining among capable, knowledgeable parties; 
22 
2) benefits ratepayers and the public interest; and 
23 
3) does not violate any important regulatory principle or practice.

Q. 
Do you have concerns with the First Stipulation regarding whether it benefits 
1 
ratepayers and the public interest? 
2 
A. 
Yes.  The First Stipulation does not adequately address the risk of cost shifting between 
3 
the customers driving significant transmission investment (new data centers) and existing 
4 
ratepayers.  There is a significant risk of stranded transmission investment and higher 
5 
transmission rates under the First Stipulation.  Therefore, the First Stipulation primarily 
6 
benefits data centers and businesses that seek to financially benefit from data centers, not 
7 
ratepayers or the public interest.   
8 
 
9 
Q. 
Do you have concerns with the First Stipulation regarding whether it violates any 
10 
important regulatory principle or practice? 
11 
A. 
Yes.  The First Stipulation shifts a large amount of risk away from data center customers 
12 
that are causing the significant change in planning and transmission investment to existing 
13 
ratepayers, including large manufacturing and industrial customers.  This could hurt job 
14 
retention.  The risk of stranded transmission investment could lead to higher transmission 
15 
rates and limit the ability of Ohio to attract large new customers with significant jobs.  This 
16 
hurts Ohio’s effectiveness in the global economy, thus violating an important regulatory 
17 
principle.

4 
Q. 
Is a general large energy intensive customer tariff more appropriate than a data 
5 
center specific tariff?  (Paragraph 2.d) 
6 
A. 
No.  Data centers are a key customer segment driving the new load growth. Given that data 
7 
centers are a significant and identified subset of the proposed EIC group with similar load 
8 
shapes (those dependent on computational and cooling needs), it would be reasonable to 
9 
group data centers together into a single schedule tariff.  As tariffs are reviewed and revised 
10 
following cost-of-service studies, grouping data centers together would reasonably assure 
11 
that the cost assigned to the class was reflective of the cost-to-serve that class.  Creating a 
12 
data center specific tariff would provide additional non-data center customer protection in 
13 
the event that data center load does not materialize and would reduce the risk of non-data 
14 
center load subsidizing data center load.  The creation of a specific data center tariff is not 
15 
unduly discriminatory in part because a separate tariff would allow for the cost allocation 
16 
and rate design to match the cost-to-serve those unique customers.  Additionally, if the data 
17 
center load does in fact materialize, then the charges assessed would be the same as if they 
18 
were served under existing tariffs. 
19

Q.

4 
First Stipulation reasonable (Paragraph 7)?

Q. 
Are the contract term and exit fee provisions in the First Stipulation appropriate?  
13 
A. 
No.  Because the exit fee follows the minimum demand charges, it acts as an extension of 
14 
the contract term and minimum demand provisions.  Per the First Stipulation, the exit fee 
15 
is only paid under Term A Agreements (not Term B or C Agreements), and the exit fee 
16 
reflects only a single year of minimum charges.  This means that the cost of terminating an 
17 
Agreement early is either small or nonexistent.

Q. 
Are the Behind-the-Meter Generation (BTMG) provisions in the First Stipulation 
21 
appropriate (Paragraph 12)?

A.
