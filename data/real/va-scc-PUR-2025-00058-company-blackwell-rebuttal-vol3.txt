Summary:
Company Witness Stan Blackwell’s testimony supports the Company’s High Load Customer 
Proposal, emphasizing its necessity due to the unprecedented growth of data centers in Virginia, 
particularly in Northern Virginia, which is the largest data center market globally. The Proposal 
aims to balance the needs of High Load Customers with the protection of the Company4 s system 
and other customers, ensuring that High Load Customers pay their fair share and do not shift costs 
to others. It includes the creation of the GS-5 tariff and rate class, minimum demand charges, a 
14-year contract term, and expanded deposit and credit requirements.
Mr. Blackwell explains that the Proposal is tailored to the unique scope and pace of High Load 
Customer load growth on the Company’s system. He notes that Staff generally supports or does 
not oppose most aspects of the Proposal and that twelve intervening parties offered a range of 
positions on the specific elements of the Proposal. He points to the diversity of views on the 
specifics and general lack of dispute that the time is right for the Proposal to be made as evidence 
that the Proposal is in fact appropriately balancing the Company’s obligation to serve High Load 
Customers while continuing to ensure proper cost allocation and protection of the Company’s 
system and other customers from stranded cost risk.
Mr. Blackwell testifies that the new GS-5 class is designed to ensure appropriate cost allocation 
for High Load Customers. He explains that the proposed 25 MW threshold and 75% load factor 
requirements are based on extensive analysis and are necessary to prevent cross-subsidization. He 
clarifies the application of the High Load Customer contract requirements and contracting process. 
He also discusses how the 14-year contract term, allowance for capacity re-sizing, and exit fee and 
collateral requirements appropriately balance High Load Customer needs, including for flexibility, 
and protection of other customers against stranded cost risks. In response to Staffs suggestion 
that the proposed collateral requirement could be subject to the Commission^ deposit rule, Mr. 
Blackwell concludes that this rule should not apply to the collateral contemplated in the Proposal, 
as the proposed requirement is fundamentally different from traditional customer deposits in 
purpose and magnitude.
Mr. Blackwell concludes by addressing Staff and Consumer Counsel recommendations that the 
capital costs associated with High Load projects that have not reached a certain degree of progress 
be removed from rate base. He emphasizes that the Company utilizes a portfolio-based approach 
such that these investments are reasonably predicted to occur. Based on his own and the 
Company’s long experience working with these customers, when one such customer falls out of 
line, another one will step into the gap given the extraordinary demand for viable locations within 
the Company’s service territory.
