I. 
INTRODUCTION 
 
 
Pursuant to 170 Ind. Admin. Code 1-1.1-22 and Ind. Code § 8-1-3-2, the Indiana Office of 
Utility Consumer Counselor (“OUCC”), by counsel, petitions the Indiana Utility Regulatory 
Commission (“Commission”) to grant rehearing and/or reconsider the Commission’s resolution of 
the issues as approved in the Order in the above-captioned Cause on June 17, 2026 (“Order”). The 
relief requested includes correcting errors by modifying the Order without further hearing based 
upon the existing record, 170 I.A.C. 1-1.1-22 (e)(3)(B), and/or should the Commission desire to 
reopen the proceeding for the receipt of further evidence under 170 I.A.C. 1-1.1-22 (e)(3)(C), the 
OUCC stands ready to do so.

Accordingly, the OUCC requests the Commission reconsider the propriety of how its 
discretion was exercised and base its determinations upon the evidence presented, without being 
swayed simply by the existence of a settlement. The Commission’s decision, including any 
settlement approval, must be supported by specific findings of fact and sufficient evidence. Order 
at 46; United States Gypsum, Inc. v. Indiana Gas Co., 735 N.E.2d 790, 795 ((Ind. 2000) (citing 
Citizens Action Coalition of Ind. v. Public Serv. Co. of Ind., Inc., 582 N.E.2d 330, 331 (Ind. 1991)  
On reconsideration, the OUCC also requests the Commission to reconsider how its 
discretion was exercised and to not exercise its discretion to the detriment of AES Indiana’s 
ratepayers by improperly weighing the importance of rate affordability (See Ind. Code § 8-1-2-
0.5) or, critically, the importance of AES Indiana tightening its budgetary belt in this second base 
rate increase since 2023. Insofar as AES Indiana justified its revenue requests by claiming they 
were consistent with what AES Indiana has historically spent, that’s a problem. That mindset 
perpetuates the rate affordability crisis instead of affirmatively tackling its resolution.  
The OUCC requests the Commission to also reconsider the legality of its findings, 
contending the Commission erred, for instance, in making ratepayers responsible for 
approximately $3 million of AES Indiana’s rate case related expenses. As discussed below, the 
Order is inherently inconsistent on this issue and demonstrates these expenses failed the “two-
tiered test” the Commission applied. 
II. 
DISCUSSION 
 
 
At the outset, the OUCC sets the record straight that while AES Indiana asserted there was 
a calculation error in the OUCC’s testimony that when “corrected” equated to the OUCC 
recommending a rate increase, this is not correct. AES Indiana’s asserted error was just that—an 
error AES Indiana claimed, but AES Indiana’s position was rejected by the OUCC as “unfounded” 
3 
 
and mistaken. (OUCC’s Brief in Support of Proposed Order (“Brief”) at 5) The OUCC stands by 
its position that AES Indiana’s current rates should be decreased. (Brief at 30) The Order does not 
lessen the OUCC’s concern about the affordability of AES Indiana’s rates or the unfortunate reality 
that these rates are not affordable for a significant portion of AES Indiana’s ratepayers. (Deupree 
Settlement Opposition Testimony at p. 25, l. 15 to p. 26, l. 8) 
The OUCC asks the Commission on reconsideration to review the issues upon which the 
OUCC took issue more closely, including AES Indiana’s rate case expenses. While rate case 
expenses are a cost of providing services, their recovery should not solely be the responsibility of 
ratepayers or a blank check for AES Indiana to spend whatever it wants, knowing ratepayers will 
be responsible.  
As noted in the Order, the Commission has explained the two-tiered test to determine 
whether rate expense is recoverable. (Order at 50) The Commission determines whether (1) the 
expense is reasonably necessary for the presentation of the case, and (2) the amount is reasonable 
in light of the service provided. (Id., See Fn. 7, citing Duke Energy Ind. LLC, Cause No. 46038 at 
73-74 (Ind. Util. Regul. Comm’n January 29, 2025)) The Commission erred in determining AES 
Indiana’s cost-of-capital expense was reasonably necessary to present its case. AES Indiana’s cost-
of-capital expense produced a component of its case-in-chief the Commission determined was 
entirely unreasonable, as there is no overlap between Mr. McKenzie’s return on equity (“ROE”) 
range of reasonableness and the Commission’s determined ROE range of reasonableness. As stated 
in the second prong of the Commission’s rate case expense test, it is squarely within the 
Commission’s discretion to determine whether any component of a utility’s proposed rate case 
expense is reasonable in light of the service provided. Mr. McKenzie’s cost component of AES 
Indiana’s rate case expense is not reasonable in light of his ROE range.  
4 
 
Further, only AES Indiana’s shareholders, not its ratepayers, could benefit from AES 
Indiana’s ROE expert in this Cause and his analysis producing an exorbitant ROE range of 10.2% 
to 11.2%, including his direct and rebuttal testimony, and related legal expense and arguments. 
The Commission rejected this analysis and ROE range as not within its reasonableness range 
(Order at 48), yet effectively ordered AES Indiana’s ratepayers to bear the costs of this analysis, 
ROE testimony, etc. Having found AES Indiana’s proposed ROE range was not reasonable (Order 
at 50), the associated expenses were not necessary to the presentation of AES Indiana’s case; 
therefore, their recovery fails the first tier of the legal test the Commission ostensibly applied. 
(Order at 50) Indiana utilities should be incented to recommend reasonable returns, not to be made 
whole financially by ratepayers when doing otherwise. AES Indiana’s proposed unreasonable ROE 
range permeated its case and warrants a sharing of AES Indiana’s rate case expenses. The 
Commission erred in not doing so. (Cf. Spire Missouri, Inc., File Nos. GR-2017-0215 and GR-
2017-0216, at 52 (Missouri Pub. Serv. Comm’n Feb. 21, 2018) (rate cases benefit the utility and 
its ratepayers; consequently, both should be responsible for rate case costs.) 
The OUCC also challenges the legality and confiscatory propriety of the Commission 
authorizing AES Indiana to continue recovering specific regulatory asset amounts from ratepayers 
after AES Indiana amortizes and fully recovers the approved amount. By analogy, lenders or other 
entities do not continue automatic account withdrawals after they are repaid the amounts owed, 
yet that is precisely what AES Indiana requested and the Commission authorized in approving the 
continued recovery of these dollars after AES Indiana fully recovers the amounts approved to do 
whatever it wants with this over-recovery. Affordability is frustrated, not furthered by this 
authorization. Importantly, other Indiana electric utilities, by agreement or order, reduce their rates 
when comparable approved amounts are fully amortized. See Petition of N. Ind. Pub. Serv. Co., 
5 
 
Cause No. 46120, Order at 12, June 26, 2025; Pet. of S. Ind. Gas & Elect. Co., Cause No. 45990, 
Order at 55, Feb. 3, 2025. Notably, in the Order in Cause No. 46171 approved on November 5, 
2025, in the City of Anderson’s rate and financing case, when the OUCC advocated against 
continuing such recovery, the Commission agreed, stating: 
The OUCC did not dispute the amount of rate case expense. However, the OUCC 
recommended the [rate case] expense be amortized over three years so that once the 
expense was fully recovered, the revenue requirement would be removed from Petitioner’s 
last phase of rate increases (i.e., Phase V). 
 
…. 
Allowing Anderson to recover an annual rate case expense after the amount has been 
fully amortized would result in the utility’s over-recovery of its expense. Accordingly, 
we find that Anderson is authorized to recover $299,500 in rate case expense. However, 
because we find four years to be a more reasonable amortization period, we find the amount 
should be amortized over four years. At the end of the amortization period, Anderson 
shall file amended tariffs removing the annual rate case expense of $74,875. (emphasis 
added) 
 
Petition of the City of Anderson, Ind., Cause No. 46173, Order at 29-30, Nov. 5, 2025. In the Order, 
the Commission did not explain why AES Indiana—unlike Anderson—should be allowed to 
continue recovering dollars earmarked for rate case expenses after these expenses are fully 
recovered. AES Indiana’s protestations against stopping this revenue stream should have been 
rejected. The Commission erred in authorizing AES Indiana to continue collecting ratepayer 
dollars earmarked for the repayment of specific regulatory assets after these amounts are fully 
recovered.   
At a time when AES Indiana should be sharpening its financial pencil, the burden of proof 
to recover expenses such as the new furniture AES Indiana included in its 2025 and 2026 estimates 
was not met by simply being “in line with historical spending.” (Holtsclaw Rebuttal, p. 4, ll. 4-8) 
Otherwise, AES witness Holtsclaw failed to support the contested new furniture expenses. (Kelley 
6 
 
Settlement Opposition Testimony, p. 6, ll. 1-5) He also provided no evidence countering OUCC 
witness Kelley’s publications reporting the Indy Eleven stadium project is not going forward. 
(Kelley Settlement Opposition Testimony, p. 6, l. 16 to p. 7, l. 10) Given this evidence, in the 
absence of AES Indiana having heard anything for over a year confirming this project is still 
planned, it has become speculative at best and should, consequently, also not have been included 
in AES Indiana’s proposed capital forecast for ratepayer recovery.  
OUCC witness Kelley also questioned the lack of support showing AES Indiana’s recloser 
expense is reasonable. He noted AES Indiana did not provide the frequency of recloser failures, 
whether repair and/or replacement is common or rare, or how frequently the warranty is not 
applicable when deriving the forecasted cost. Mr. Holtsclaw, in his settlement rebuttal testimony, 
acknowledged “the forecast considers historical experience and known information about the 
projected period,” (Holtsclaw Settlement Rebuttal, p. 16, l. 20 to p. 17, l. 1), but he provided no 
itemized costs or calculations for the forecasted repair and reinstallation recloser costs or insight 
into how that historical experience was weighed or mitigated. He simply opined that “the 
Company’s forecast rests of [sic] a sound factual and analytical foundation.” (Holtsclaw Settlement 
Rebuttal, p. 18, ll. 19-20) The precise nature of these sound factual and analytical underpinnings 
remains unknown; consequently, AES Indiana failed to meet its burden of proof with respect to 
the forecasted recloser amount, and the Commission erred in its approval. 
Other issues, as discussed and identified below, include, but are not limited to: 
(1) Base Cost of Fuel. The base cost of fuel of $0.04381 per kWh the Commission approved 
under the modified Settlement Agreement is higher than OUCC witness Eckert’s recommended 
$0.042260 per kWh. The Commission approved the higher base cost of fuel without findings 
explaining this determination (Order at 53), or why a higher fuel cost than the OUCC proposed 
7 
 
should be embedded in AES Indiana’s base rates. AES Indiana’s current base cost of fuel, as agreed 
and approved in Cause No. 45911, is $0.039027 per kWh. As the OUCC advocated in this Cause, 
AES Indiana’s ratepayers should be protected from, not subject to, higher base fuel rates because 
AES Indiana can use the FAC to recover a variability in the utility’s fuel costs, without base rates 
being higher and less affordable. While AES Indiana witness Rogers characterized the approved 
settlement adjustment as a “reasonable compromise on this issue” (Rogers Settlement Testimony 
at 24, ll. 13-14), the OUCC urges the Commission to prioritize affordability and adopt the OUCC’s 
requested base cost of fuel, with any variability in fuel costs recovered via AES Indiana’s FAC 
filings instead of up front from consumers in base rates. (Brief at 4-5)  
(2) 2024 Major Storm Cost Regulatory Liability Balance. OUCC witness Lantrip proposed 
that the remaining regulatory liability balance AES Indiana over-collected as a Major Storm Cost 
and the Commission ordered amortized in May 2024 in Cause No. 45911 be used as an immediate, 
one-time offset of $4.431 million to the regulatory asset balance increase ASE Indiana sought. This 
liability balance was originally $6.1 million. In this Cause, AES Indiana sought a $7.335 million 
increase to its major Storm Regulatory Asset balance. Per AES Indiana witness Coklow, the 
original $6.1 million credit “is slowly being wound down, consistent with the accounting approved 
in Cause No. 45911.” (Order at 25) The Commission accepted but failed to explain why this over-
collection from consumers should continue to be slowly wound down as opposed to approving the 
one-time offset Mr. Lantrip proposed.   
(3) ROE & ROE Adjustments. The Commission found the reasonable range for AES 
Indiana’s ROE is 9.1% to 9.9% (Order at 48) but approved 9.5% instead of 9.1% notwithstanding 
the significant evidence upon AES Indiana’s quality of service and affordability challenges and 
without addressing why 9.1% was not reasonable given the record.  
8 
 
OUCC witness Courter recommended a 9.0% ROE (before affordability and quality of 
service adjustments). Walmart’s witness Lyon testified the range of reported authorized ROEs 
nationally for the recent period is 8.63% to 11.45%. (Order at 14) The AES Indiana Industrial 
Group recommended an ROE within a range of 9.1% to 9.90%. (Order at 11) The Commission 
acknowledged receiving more than 7,000 consumer comments relaying how AES Indiana’s current 
bills negatively impact their daily lives, concerns about the potential effect of the proposed rate 
increase, the adverse issues customers have endured because of AES Indiana’s new billing system, 
and the difficulty ratepayers and the Commission’s Consumer Affairs Division encounter when 
attempting to resolve customer complaints. (Order at 49) At the peak of ratepayers’ adverse 
impacts from the new billing system, more than 12%, or approximately 65,000 of AES Indiana’s 
customers were affected. (Hearing Tr. Vol. F 6:25 to 7:14, January 29, 2026) Thousands of other 
customers were also affected during and after the rollout. AES Indiana witness Rogers 
acknowledged the Commission has used the ROE at the lower end of the reasonable ROE range, 
in this case 9.1%, to reflect the Commission’s displeasure with a utility’s management and 
encourage improved future performance. (Order at 16) The Commission erred in not doing so in 
this Cause. The Commission also erred in not addressing why 9.1% was within the range of 
reasonableness but not reasonable for AES Indiana, especially with the Commission having 
rejected the OUCC’s 50 basis point adjustments for affordability and quality of service.  
The OUCC also requests the Commission reconsider whether the affordability pillar has, 
indeed, been adequately addressed without the OUCC’s proposed ROE adjustment incorporated. 
The Commission viewed the financial impact and repercussions flowing from the ACE Project 
billing system debacle as “revenue concessions” benefitting the residential class (Order at 57), but 
9 
 
these financial ramifications were of AES Indiana’s own doing. They should not be equated with 
addressing customers’ affordability and/or service concerns. (Order at 57) 
(4) Uncollectible Expense. Per the Commission, AES Indiana witness Coklow testified the 
methodology used to calculate uncollectible expense is consistent with the settlements approved 
in Cause Nos. 45029, 45911, and 44576.1 (Order at 27) OUCC witness Eckert calculated this 
expense by taking the uncollectible account percentages for the last ten years and removing the 
two highest years, 2021 and 2023, because these two years were significantly higher than the eight-
year average, 0.3994%, as compared to 0.4992% for 2023 and 0.4707% for 2021. (Eckert Direct, 
p. 22, Table MDE-8) Mr. Eckert did not remove any lower percentage years because no years were 
significantly lower. Essentially, he removed the two years that were outliers.  
The Commission erred in summarily accepting the uncollectible accounts expense 
calculation in the settlement based upon that calculation being “consistent with Commission 
precedent” and substantial evidence reflective of prospective utility operations. (Order at 54) The 
prior settlements Ms. Coklow referenced—and the “Commission precedent” referenced—
preclude their provisions from “establish[ing] any principles or legal precedent applicable to 
Commission proceedings.” (Petition of Indianapolis Power & Light Co., Cause No. 45029, IURC 
Order approved Oct. 31, 2018, attached Stipulation & Settlement Agreement at 14; Petition of 
Indianapolis Power & Light Co. d/b/a AES Ind., Cause No. 45911, IURC Order approved April 
18, 20244, attached Stipulation & Settlement Agreement at 11-12). The Commission’s findings 
approving the uncollectible accounts expense are, consequently, legally deficient and infirm. 
(5) Vacant Payroll Positions. The Commission approved Section I.A.11 of the Settlement 
Agreement without findings explaining this determination or evidencing the Commission

considered all the evidence. Indiana appellate courts have upheld the importance of such findings 
because they provide enlightenment as to the agency’s reasoning process. Additionally, requiring 
basic findings assists the agency in avoiding arbitrary, capricious, or ill-considered action. Nextel 
West Corp. v. Indiana Util. Regulatory Comm’n, 831 N.E.2d 134, 144 (Ind. Ct. App. 2005)  
The approved payroll settlement adjustment is less than half of OUCC witness Baker’s 
adjustment and inexplicably ignores the consistent number of vacant positions AES Indiana had at 
the time of its last base rate case, Cause No. 45911, and this Cause. Per AES Indiana’s discovery 
responses, as of June 26, 2025, AES Indiana had 94 vacant positions, and as of July 15, 2025, 115 
vacant positions. (Baker Revised Testimony, IURC Public’s Ex. 4, p. 9) In Cause No. 45911, AES 
Indiana reported having 105 vacant positions. (Id. at 10) Thus, notwithstanding the efforts AES 
Indiana witness Dalton described to fill vacant positions (Order at 27), ratepayers have been 
bankrolling and AES Indiana has been recovering millions of dollars in compensation and 
expenses for 100-plus vacant positions since rates were approved in Cause No. 46911 on April 17, 
2024. While positions may be filled, the ebb and flow of vacancies has remained about the same. 
AES Indiana’s compensation windfall of more than $15 million (Baker Revised Testimony, IURC 
Public’s Ex. 4, at 12), at ratepayers’ expense should stop, but the approved settlement adjustment 
of only $4 million (Order, Exhibit 1 at 3, ¶ 11) does not do so. The Commission erred in not 
providing sufficient findings and in perpetuating AES Indiana’s over-recovery at ratepayers’ 
expense of payroll and related amounts, thereby not protecting the affordability of utility services 
for AES Indiana’s consumers. 
(6) Decommissioning Cost Study Contingency. OUCC witness Garren recommended using 
a 10% contingency for retirement costs in the decommissioning estimate as opposed to the 20% 
contingency AES Indiana requested. Mr. Garren’s recommendation would have provided annual 
11 
 
ratepayer savings of approximately $10 million as compared to AES Indiana’s requested 
contingency. (Brief at 19; Garren Settlement Testimony, Public Exhibit No. 9-S at 9) The 
Commission approved the 20% contingency factor in the decommissioning cost estimate, 
providing only the following findings: 
The Commission has also held that the inclusion of contingency is appropriate. The 
20% contingency factor in the Settlement Agreement is consistent with industry 
standard and consistent with AES Indiana’s two previous rate cases. … Therefore, 
the Commission finds the Settlement Agreement depreciation rates are reasonable 
and represent a fair compromise on all the parties’ positions concerning updated 
AES Indiana’s depreciation rates. (footnote omitted) 
 
Order at 47.  
As discussed above, AES Indiana’s two prior rate cases, Cause Nos. 45029 and 45911, 
were resolved by settlement. In both cases, the Commission directed that the settlement agreement 
“is not to be used as precedent in any other proceeding or for any other purpose except to the 
extent necessary to implement or enforce its terms … .” (emphasis added); (45029 Order at 33; 
45911 Order at 32) The Commission erred insofar as its findings approving AES Indiana’s 20% 
contingency factor rest upon the prior settlements and are, therefore, contrary to the legal principle 
the Commission endorsed in Richmond Power & Light, Cause No. 40434, 1997 WL 34880849 at 
7-8 (IURC March 19, 1997) and in the 45029 and 45911 Orders. The cursory reference to industry 
standards and the prior settlements do not demonstrate the Commission relied on substantial 
evidence presented in this Cause.  
The OUCC requests the Commission reconsider its decision and reduce the level of 
contingency included in the decommissioning cost study to 10%, thereby providing ratepayers’ 
annual savings. As Mr. Garren testified, he has never reviewed a decommissioning study for an 
electric or gas plant that used a contingency factor over 20%. (Garren Direct, p. 37, ll. 2-3) Thus, 
AES Indiana’s 20% is at the highest end of Mr. Garren’s experience. He also noted that 
12 
 
decommissioning studies are highly detailed studies based on specific considerations of each plant, 
with detailed estimations of labor, scrap, and other considerations; consequently, such higher 
degree of detail supports the proposed lower contingency factor. 
III. 
CONCLUSION 
WHEREFORE, the OUCC respectfully requests the Commission to: (a) reconsider and 
reverse the Order insofar as the Commission erroneously approved the Settlement Agreement, as 
modified; or (b) modify the Order (and by virtue of doing so, further modify the Settlement 
Agreement) by correcting the errors identified above and modifying the Commission’s resolution 
of the issues upon which the Commission exercised its discretion to the detriment of AES Indiana’s 
ratepayers by more properly weighing rate affordability on reconsideration and holding AES 
Indiana accountable for tightening its budgetary belt; or (c) if the Commission determines its 
review would be facilitated by reopening the proceedings for further evidence on particular issues, 
that the Commission upon notice to the parties reopen the proceedings, and (d) for such further 
relief as the Commission deems appropriate.
