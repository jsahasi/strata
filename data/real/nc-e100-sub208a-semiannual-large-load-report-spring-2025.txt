Semi-Annual Update Report on Large-Load Customer Additions in Advanced 
Stages of Development – Spring 2025 

Duke Energy Carolinas, LLC (“DEC”) and Duke Energy Progress, LLC (“DEP” 
and together with DEC, “the Companies”) are filing this semi-annual large load addition 
report (the “Report”) with North Carolina Utilities Commission (“Commission”) pursuant 
to the Commission’s Order Accepting Stipulation, Granting Partial Waiver of Commission 
Rule R8-60A(d)(4), and Providing Further Direction for Future Planning, Docket No. E-
100, Sub 190 (Nov. 1, 2024) (the “2023 CPIRP Order”).  Specifically, Ordering Paragraph 
No.  7  directed  the  Companies  to  “monitor  continued  economic  development  and  the 
pipeline of large load additions (at or greater than 20 MW), including wholesale load, in 
their service territories and . . . file semiannual reports with the Commission addressing 
changes to proposed large load additions in the advanced stage of development.”1    

Background 

In  the  2023  Carbon  Plan  and  Integrated  Resource  Plan  (“CPIRP”)  and  2024 
Supplemental  Planning Analysis  (“SPA”),  the  Companies  identified  that  their  historical 
load  forecasting  approach  likely  was  not  capturing  the  rapid  and  unprecedented  large 
customer  load  growth  that  was  occurring  due  to  the  Carolinas  economic  development 
success.2  Accordingly, the Companies incorporated an economic development adjustment 
to their Spring 2023 Load Forecast, which was used to develop the CPIRP.  

To  prepare  the  economic  development  adjustment,  the  Companies  identify 
proposed  projects  with  large  loads  in  an  advanced  stage  of  development.    Because  the 
economic development process is competitive and numerous factors introduce uncertainty 
and can impact whether a large load addition will actually come to fruition, the Companies 
only  consider  proposed  new  large  load  projects  that  are  sufficiently  advanced  in 
development  and  have  made  commitments  towards  taking  electric  service  from  the 
Companies  such  that  it  is  reasonable  to  include  such  project  in  the  Companies’  load 
forecast.  Specifically, to be included in the economic development adjustment, potential 
customers must have:  

(1) Executed  an  Electric  Service Agreement  (“ESA”)  detailing  the  service  to  be
provided, the Rate Tariff and any Riders in effect, any extra facilities provided
and their associated costs, and the term of the agreement;

(2) Executed  a  letter  agreement  formalizing  the  location,  size,  and  nature  of  the
load  and  the  load  ramp  timing  and  committing  the  potential  customer  to

Once a project  meets  the above advanced development criteria, it is then considered an 
“Advanced  Development  Project”  and  the  Companies  incorporate  a  discounted  load 
projection for purposes of developing the updated load forecast.3   

The Companies initially identified eight (8) Advanced Development Projects with 
an annual “full load” peak demand of approximately 1,350 MW for incorporation into the 
2023 Spring Load Forecast which was used as an input into the 2023 CPIRP.  In preparing 
the 2023 Fall Load Forecast, the Companies identified an additional 27 projects that had 
made sufficient commitments to qualify as Advanced Development Projects for inclusion 
in  the  updated  load  forecast.  Thus,  a  total  of  35  Advanced  Development  Projects—
collectively 3,883 MW of full load peak demand requirements—were included in the first 
update to the Companies’ economic development adjustment used in preparing the SPA 
load forecast filed on January 31, 2024. The Companies last updated the Commission on 
the  status  of  economic  development  in  their  service  territories  through  the  Rebuttal 
Testimony of Phil Stillman, Andrew Tate, and Chris Edge, filed on July 1, 2024, reporting 
the identification of 39 Advanced Development Projects totaling just over 4,000 MW of 
full load peak demand requirements (“June 2024 Update”). 

In  its  2023  CPIRP  Order,  the  Commission  acknowledged  that  the  Companies’ 
service  territories  in  North  Carolina  and  South  Carolina  “are  experiencing  significant 
economic development activity [that] materially impacts Duke’s load forecast.”4 In light of 
such  a  significant  impact,  the  Commission  found  that  the  Companies’  method  for 
developing their load forecast, including use of the Economic Development Adjustment, 
was  reasonable  for  planning  purposes  and  directed  the  Companies  to  file  semiannual 
reports providing detail on economic development activity and large load customer growth 
in its service areas.5

As  a  general  matter,  the  current  economic  development  environment  in  the 
Carolinas  continues  to  be  dynamic  and  unprecedented  in  terms  of  the  number  of  new 
businesses seeking to locate in the Companies’ service territories in North Carolina and 
South Carolina. Since the Companies’ June 2024 Update, the Companies have continued 
to receive inquiries from and on behalf of potential customers at a scale and pace that is 
well beyond their historical experience. These most recent inquiries reflect projects across 
a wide range of industries at varying stages of development, from very early discussions 
and  generic  information  gathering  through  advanced  site  and  project  development.    In 
addition, four advanced stage projects that were previously captured and included in prior 
load forecast updates have now achieved commercial operation.    Certain other projects 
previously categorized as advanced development have reduced size or have delayed their 
plans to take electric service from the Companies and are no longer designated Advanced 
Development Projects.   

  While  the  total  number  of  Advanced  Development  Projects  have  remained

portfolio  of  Advanced  Development  Projects  has  increased  by  approximately  50%  or 
nearly 2,000 MW on a full load projection basis as compared to the June 2024 Update.  Of

In  addition  to  the  foregoing  projections  of  large  load  customers  additions  to  be 
directly  served  by  the  Companies  in  their  North  Carolina  and  South  Carolina  service 
territories, the Companies also plan for potential large load customer additions served by 
electric cooperative or municipal utility customers who purchase power at wholesale from 
DEC or DEP. In order to plan for and include wholesale large load customer requirements 
in the forecast, the Companies work directly with many of the wholesale customers that 
are served by DEC and DEP.  These wholesale customer forecasts are subject to many of 
the  same  economic  trends  included  in  the  retail  forecast  including  likely  economic 
development  additions.   Currently,  the  Companies’  wholesale  forecast  includes  two  (2) 
discrete projects that ramp up over the planning period, and total nearly 900 MW by 2035.  
The Companies will continue to engage with wholesale customers to identify potential new 
large load customers that are progressing towards advanced stages of development.
