 7                   prices and on the other expenses required by a business to operate. As such, the

 2       (A)   Revenue Requirement and ROE

 3         Q.  WHAT IS YOUR UNDERSTANDING OF THE COMPANY'S PROPOSED

 4           REVENUE REQUIREMENT INCREASE?

 5         A.   My understanding is that the Company is seeking an annual revenue increase of

 6                 approximately $226.1 million, or 11.5 percent.3 This proposed increase is based on

 7                 a 12-month forecasted test period ending December 31, 2026,4 as supported by a

 8               12-month base period ending August 31, 2025, and consisting of six months of

 9                   actual data (September 1, 2024 through February 28, 2025) and six months of

10                  forecasted data (March 1, 2025 through August 31, 2025).5

11         Q.  WHAT IS THE COMPANY'S PROPOSED ROE IN THIS CASE?

12         A.    The Company proposes an ROE of 10.95 percent based on a range of 10.32 percent

13                   to 11.84 percent.6 In addition, the Company proposes a capital structure of 46.15

14                  percent debt and 52.86 percent equity for ratemaking purposes.7 Applying the

15               Company's proposed ROE to its proposed capital structure, the Company is asking

16                  the Commission to approve a Rate of Return equal to 8.10 percent based on 13-

17               month weighted average cost of capital ("WACC").8

 3         Q.   ARE THERE INSTANCES IN WHICH THE COMMISSION WOULD

 7         A.     Yes. At times, the regulator may find it necessary to approve a level of revenue

 9                 determined in the COSS. This is often driven by the need to ensure that customers

10                   are not seriously adversely impacted by major changes to the level of rates. Other

11                  reasons can include perceived differences in COSS results and reality, relative risks

12                 assigned to classes, social goals associated with the role of the prices in a particular

13                    jurisdiction, and response to the state of the economy within or external to the

14                  regulatory jurisdiction. The Commission may exercise its discretion based on one

15                  or more of these concerns to adjust revenue allocation to support policy or advance

16                  the public interest. However, these adjustments often result in rates that are not

17                  cost-based and, as a result, may not be just, reasonable, and equitable.

22                  of undesired subsidies, continually moving each class closer to their respective cost

23                  of service until undesired subsidies are eliminated and price signals are improved.

 7                 communities, particularly during storms and other emergency events. For example,

 8                   in the event of a power outage during a storm, customers need a knowledgeable and

 9                    efficient point of contact to coordinate power restoration, advocate and direct action

10                  within the utility, and work to ensure business continuity. If one of Walmart's stores

11                   loses power, knowing the estimated duration of the outage in a timely manner can

12                 help determine whether Walmart should provide a mobile generator to operate the

13                    entire facility or whether refrigerated trailers are needed to refrigerate perishables

14                and how soon Walmart can return to serving the community. Additionally, the

15                  process of installing a mobile generator requires utility engagement – utility crews

16                   are required to pull fuses on the distribution system in order to allow the generator

17                   to interconnect – and to execute on this process efficiently requires the attention

18                and expertise of an account representative. Repeatedly calling a call center, which

19             may involve speaking with multiple different representatives, providing identifying

20                   info (account number,  service  address,  etc.), reviewing information already

21                  provided, and not knowing when and how the issue will be resolved is inefficient

22                and frustrating for customers.
