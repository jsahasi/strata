12             III. AMEREN MISSOURI'S LARGE LOAD RATE PLAN - BACKGROUND

13         Q.   What were the key drivers behind the design of the Large Load Rate Plan?

14         A.    There are two primary drivers. First, we have an obligation to serve those who

15    desire to locate in our service territory.  This includes individuals and businesses of any kind.

16   Given the uniqueness of these Large Load Customers, it made sense to develop tariff terms tailored

17    to the needs and characteristics of these kinds of customers while ensuring reliability and fair rates

18    for all customers.  Second, these kinds of loads present significant economic development

19    opportunities and benefits for Ameren Missouri's service territory and for the State of Missouri

20    overall. The new terms and conditions of service reflected in the Plan will aid us in capturing

21    those benefits.

 1         Q.   What  are  some  of  the  challenges  regulated  utilities  are  facing  in

 9    utilities are mindful of the need for these new customers to pay their fair share of costs and of the

10   importance of maintaining just and reasonable rates for all customers.

 1           IV.  AMEREN MISSOURI'S LARGE LOAD RATE PLAN – SPECIFICS

 2         Q.    Please describe the Ameren Missouri Large Load Rate Plan and its key

 3   elements.

 4         A.    As outlined in Company witness Wills' Direct Testimony, Ameren Missouri is

 5    creating a new subclass within its existing 11(M) classification to apply to customers with an actual

 6    or expected demand greater than or equal to 100 MW, and that requires such customers to take

 7    service at transmission voltages, i.e., at service voltages of 115 kV or greater.

 8         The Large Load Customers that fall within this new sub class would be subject to additional

 9   terms and conditions. Those additional terms and conditions build on an already established and

10    successful ratemaking framework, in order to provide assurance of an adequate long-term revenue

11    stream, to support accelerating the investment in generating assets the Company had in most cases

12    previously planned to add  later and that will be needed  earlier to serve these customers.

13    Specifically, Large Load Customers will be required to enter into an Electric Service Agreement

14   ("ESA”) with the Company with the following key terms, which must be approved by the

15   Commission:

16            •  Large Load Customers must contractually commit to a term of service of at least

17            15 years. The term will be broken down into a ramp period and a full load

18               period. The ramp period may be selected by the customer and may extend for

19            up to 5 years. The full load period will commence immediately upon conclusion

20              of the ramp period and will extend for an additional 12 years. 

21            •  Large Load Customers must commit to a minimum level of demand charges to

22             be reflected on their monthly bill for the term of their ESA that is equal to 70%


                                           14      Direct Testimony of
     Ajay K. Arora

 1              of the contracted capacity reflected in their ESA.7 The applicable contract

 2              capacity will be spelled out for the customer's ramp period in the ESA and there

 3                shall be a maximum contracted capacity agreed between the customer and the

 4            Company.

 5            •  Large Load Customers must either meet certain credit ratings, or else post

 6                collateral significant security in the form of cash, a suitable letter of credit, or a

 7              guaranty from a creditworthy affiliate.

 8            •  Large Load Customers may terminate their ESA but only effective 24 months

 9                after they have given written notice to the Company, and if they do so they will

10             be subject to substantial termination payments. The termination payments must

11              cover their minimum demand obligations for the remainder of the ramp period

12                   (if termination incurs during the ramp period) plus their minimum demand

13              charge obligations post-the ramp period for the lesser of five years or the then

14             remaining term of their ESA (if less than five years), plus their clean energy

15            program commitments (discussed further below) over the same period.

16            •  Large Load Customers have the ability to participate in four new programs that

17               are specifically designed to assist them in meeting their clean or carbon-free

18             energy goals.
