Semi-Annual Update Report on Large-Load Customer Additions in Advanis ced 
Stages of Development – Fall 2025 

Duke Energy Carolinas, LLC (“DEC”) and Duke Energy Progress, LLC (“DEP” 
and together with DEC, “the Companies”) are filing this semi-annual large load addition 
report (the “Fall 2025 Report”) with North Carolina Utilities Commission (“Commission”) 
pursuant  to  the  Commission’s  Order Accepting  Stipulation,  Granting  Partial  Waiver  of 
Commission  Rule  R8-60A(d)(4),  and  Providing  Further  Direction  for  Future  Planning, 
Docket  No.  E-100,  Sub  190  (Nov.  1,  2024)  (the  “2024  CPIRP  Order”).    Specifically, 
Ordering  Paragraph  No.  7  directed  the  Companies  to  “monitor  continued  economic 
development and the pipeline of large load additions (at or greater than 20 MW), including 
wholesale  load,  in  their  service  territories  and  .  .  .  file  semiannual  reports  with  the 
Commission addressing changes to proposed large load additions in the advanced stage of 
development.”1   This  Report  provides  the  second  semi-annual  Report  filed  pursuant  to 
Ordering  Paragraph  No.  7.    The  first  semi-annual  Report  was  filed  on  May  15,  2025 
(“Spring 2025 Report”).   

Background 

The  Companies  first  developed  and  incorporated  an  economic  development 
adjustment  into  their  Spring  2023  Load  Forecast  (which  was  used  to  develop  the  2023 
Carbon Plan and Integrated Resource Plan (“CPIRP”)) and Fall 2023 Load Forecast (which 
was  used  to  develop  the  2024  Supplemental  Planning Analysis  (“SPA”)).    In  its  2024 
CPIRP  Order,  the  Commission  acknowledged  that  the  Companies’  service  territories  in 
North Carolina and South Carolina “are experiencing significant economic development 
activity  [that]  materially  impacts  Duke’s  load  forecast.”2  In  light  of  such  a  significant 
impact,  the  Commission  found  that  the  Companies’  method  for  developing  their  load 
forecast, including the use of the Economic Development Adjustment, was reasonable for 
planning purposes and directed the Companies to file semiannual reports providing detail 
on economic development activity and large load customer growth in its service areas.3  As 
presented in their recently filed 2025 CPIRP in this docket, the Companies are continuing 
to apply an economic development adjustment to  their load forecast to ensure that they 
capture  the  full  expected  impact  of  large  load-related  economic  development  in  the 
Carolinas as part of their resource plan. 

The Companies’ economic development adjustment to the load forecast recognizes 
that  only  a  fraction  of  their  overall  economic  development  pipeline  will  ultimately 
materialize. Therefore,  the  load  forecasting  process  is  designed  to  account  for  the  most 
mature  and  committed  projects  classified  as  “Advanced  Development”  while  also 
recognizing, based on the Companies’ recent experience, that a portion of the additional

Advanced  Development  Projects  are  defined  as  projects  that  have  sufficiently 
advanced plans such that their demand can be anticipated with a high degree of certainty. 
To be considered an Advanced Development Project, potential customers must generally 
have:  

(1) Executed  an  Electric  Service Agreement  (“ESA”)  detailing  the  service  to  be

provided;

(2) Executed a Letter Agreement (“LA”) formalizing the location, size, and nature
of the load and the load ramp timing and committing the potential customer to
reimburse the Companies for money spent designing or constructing facilities
on behalf of the customer; or

(3) Progressed to advanced stage discussions in advance of signing an LA or ESA

(the “Late-Stage Pipeline”).4

In  the  2023  Spring  Load  Forecast,  the  Companies  initially  identified  eight  (8) 
Advanced  Development  Projects  with  an  annual  “full  load”  peak  demand5  of 
approximately 1,350 MW for incorporation into the 2023 Spring Load Forecast which was 
used as an input into the 2023 CPIRP.  In preparing the 2023 Fall Load Forecast (used for 
the 2024 SPA), the Companies identified an additional 27 projects that had made sufficient 
commitments to qualify as Advanced Development Projects for inclusion in the Fall 2023 
Load  Forecast. Thus, a total of  35 Advanced Development  Projects—collectively  3,833 
MW of full load peak demand requirements by 2034—were included in the 2024 SPA.  

As  reported  in  the  Spring  2025  Report,  the  Companies  identified  a  total  of  35 
Advanced Development Projects for inclusion in the Spring 2025 Load Forecast economic 
development  adjustment—collectively  5,914 MW of  peak demand  requirements (at  full 
load) by the tenth year of the forecast.  The Spring 2025 Load Forecast is the forecast used 
for the 2025 CPIRP.

In  addition  to  the  foregoing  projections  of  large  load  customers  additions  to  be 
served  at  retail  by  the  Companies  in  their  North  Carolina  and  South  Carolina  service 
territories, the Companies also plan for potential large load customer additions served by 
electric cooperative or municipal utility customers who purchase power at wholesale from 
DEC or DEP.  In order to plan for and include wholesale large load customer requirements 
in the forecast, the Companies work directly with the wholesale customers that are served 
by DEC and DEP.  These wholesale customer forecasts are subject to many of the same 
economic  trends  included  in  the  retail  forecast  including  likely  economic  development 
additions.  Currently, the Companies’ wholesale forecast includes two (2) discrete projects 
that ramp up over the planning period, and total nearly 900 MW by 2035.  The Companies 
will  continue  to  engage  with  wholesale  customers  to  identify  potential  new  large  load 
customers that are progressing towards advanced stages of development.
