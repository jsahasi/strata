I.  Overview and Background 

Spring 2026 

Duke  Energy  is  committed  to  supporting  economic  vitality  in  the  Carolinas  by 
collaborating with current and prospective customers and communities to understand and plan for 
future energy needs.  The current economic development environment in the Carolinas continues 
to be dynamic in terms of the number and size of new businesses seeking to locate in the Duke 
Energy Carolinas, LLC (“DEC”) and Duke Energy Progress, LLC (“DEP” and together with DEC, 
the “Companies”) service territories.   

The  most  notable  development  reflected  in  this  Spring  2026  Large  Load  Report  is  the 
substantial number of large load projects that are seeking to invest in North and South Carolina 
and  have  progressed  to  executed  Electric  Service Agreements  (“ESA”).    Specifically,  the  total 
number of large load projects with executed ESAs has increased to 16 projects and with a total of 
4,332 MW.   

As further  background  regarding the  genesis and purpose of this report, the Companies 
identified  in  the  development  of  the  2023 Carolinas  Resource  Plan  that  their  econometric  load 
forecasting approach was not capturing the rapid and unprecedented large load customer growth 
occurring due to economic development success in the Carolinas.  Accordingly, the Companies 
developed and incorporated an Economic Development Adjustment into their Spring 2023 Load 
Forecast  which  was  used  to  develop  the  2023  Carolinas  Resource  Plan.   After  filing  the  2023 
Carolinas Resource Plan, the Companies further identified that initial representation of economic 
development did not fully capture the continued strong growth in economic development interest 
in  the  Carolinas,  and  therefore  filed  a  Supplemental  Planning Analysis  to  the  2023  Carolinas 
Resource Plan to assess the impacts of such growth on the resource needs in the Carolinas. 

In light of the sustained economic development activity, the Companies are continuing to 
apply an Economic Development Adjustment to their load forecast to ensure that they continue to 
capture the projected impact of large load economic development in the Carolinas as part of their 
resource planning, including the 2025 Carolinas Resource Plan filed in North Carolina and the 
2025  Integrated  Resource  Plan  Update  filed  in  South  Carolina.   This  Spring  2026  Large  Load 
Report  provides  an  update  on  large  load  projects  that  are  currently  in  advanced  stages  of 
development and will be incorporated into the Economic Development Adjustment applied to the 
Spring 2026 Load Forecast.  

The Companies’ Economic Development Adjustment is designed to account for both the 
more mature and committed large load projects—which are referred to as “Advanced Development 
Projects”—while also recognizing, based on the Companies’ recent experience, that a portion of 
the  additional  large  load  economic  development  projects  earlier  in  the  pipeline  are  likely  to 
advance.   

Advanced Development Projects are those potential customers that have:

(3) Progressed to advanced stage discussions in advance of signing a Letter Agreement or 

ESA (the “Late-Stage Pipeline”).    

As  detailed  below,  this  Spring  2026  Large  Load  Report  identifies  43  Advanced 
Development Projects—collectively 7,946 MW of peak demand requirements (at full load) by the 
tenth year of the forecast—demonstrating continued growth as projects advance through the large 
load pipeline.  This total amount includes those projects that have progressed to execution of ESAs 
as noted above.      

The Companies also monitor projects at earlier stages of the process, and the Economic 
Development Adjustment  also  recognizes  that  a  portion  of  these  projects  are  likely  to  advance 
through the process to execute an ESA.  For purposes of the 2025 Carolinas Resource Plan, the 
Companies captured this future economic development pipeline growth by assuming the additional 
materialization of approximately 2,000 MW by 2040 as part of their base portfolio which has now 
come  to  fruition.    Importantly,  the  Companies  continue  to  receive  strong  indicia  of  additional 
economic  development  interest  from  potential  customers,  including  through  the  Companies’ 
updated  large  load  queue  process.  Through  this  process,  the  Companies  continue  to  engage 
customers in  a disciplined and structured  manner, and  the level of  interest in receiving service 
remains strong and the scale of such interest is significant.

In addition to the foregoing projections of Advanced Development Projects to be served at 
retail by the Companies in their Carolinas’ service territories, the Companies also plan for potential 
large load customer additions served by electric cooperative or municipal utility customers who 
purchase power at wholesale from DEC or DEP.  In order to plan for and include wholesale large 
load  customer  requirements  in  the  forecast,  the  Companies  work  directly  with  the  wholesale 
customers that are served by DEC and DEP.  These wholesale customer forecasts are subject to 
many  of  the  same  economic  trends  included  in  the  retail  forecast  including  likely  economic 
development  additions.   Currently,  the  Companies’  wholesale  forecast  includes  two  discrete 
projects that ramp up over the planning period, and total nearly 900 MW by 2035.    The Companies 
will continue to engage with wholesale customers to identify potential new large load customers 
that are progressing towards advanced stages of development.
