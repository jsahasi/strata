 88   Q.  WHAT IS EXCESS LIABILITY INSURANCE?

 89   A.    ELI provides coverage for liability to third parties arising out of bodily injury and

 90           property damage losses suffered by those third parties. It includes coverage for general

 91             liability, wildfire liability, and employer’s liability. Claims for damages to third parties

 92           are included within excess liability coverage.


 93   Q.  HOW DOES RMP ACQUIRE ITS ELI COVERAGE?

 94   A.     PacifiCorp d/b/a RMP, through  its parent company, Berkshire Hathaway Energy

 95        Company (“BHE”), purchases ELI as part of BHE’s aggregated insurance purchase.

 96         The ELI premiums are allocated to PacifiCorp and other BHE subsidiaries through

 97        BHE’s corporate allocation, which is based on an average percentage of: property,

 98            plant, and equipment; employee count; loss history; overhead electric T&D lines; and

 99      T&D pipeline miles.3

103           Utah-allocated basis using the System Overhead allocation factor from the 2020 Multi-

104           State Cost Allocation Protocol.

                                         Page 11                                    III. INSURANCE COST ADJUSTMENT


194   Q.  WHY IS RMP PROPOSING TO IMPLEMENT THE ICA RIDER?

195   A.   RMP states that the proposed ICA rider would allow the Company to track and recover

196         ELI costs and collections, separate and apart from base rates.  Additionally, the

197        Company plans to use this mechanism to recover other as-yet undefined insurance

198            related costs, and potentially combine commercial insurance and Company self-

199           insurance in this rider.13


200   Q.   ARE THERE ANY PROBLEMS WITH THE PROPOSED ICA RIDER?

201   A.     Yes.  First, the proposed ICA rider constitutes single-issue or piecemeal ratemaking.

202          Second, it is unclear whether the proposed ICA rider would only recover insurance

203          premiums. According to RMP’s responses to data requests, the Company would also

204           include punitive damage insurance policy costs in the proposed ICA rider.14 However,

205           the proposed rider does not have a list or any description of the specific costs that would

206          be eligible for recovery. Third, RMP’s proposal to spread the costs across classes using

207          base revenues is not consistent with cost-causation principles.


208   Q.  WHAT  DO  YOU  MEAN  BY  SINGLE-ISSUE  OR  PIECEMEAL

209       RATEMAKING?

210   A.    Piecemeal ratemaking occurs whenever a utility is allowed to implement a tracker that



          13 Amended Direct Testimony of Joelle R. Steward, p. 6, lines 114-120.
          14 RMP Response to OCS Data Request 10.3, a copy of which is included in Exhibit BSL-3.

           Direct Testimony of                                        Docket No. 24-035-04
            Billie S. LaConte                                     Utah Large Customer Group

                                         Page 12211           guarantees dollar-for-dollar cost recovery.  Piecemeal ratemaking allows a utility to

212           adjust rates outside of, and absent the scrutiny provided in, a general rate case. Such

213           guarantees are contrary to the regulatory compact in which a utility should be allowed

214          a reasonable opportunity to earn a reasonable rate of return on the facilities used to

215          provide electricity service and to recover reasonable and necessary operating expenses.

216        When costs are evaluated in a general rate case, they are considered comprehensively,

217          whereas a tracker, such as the proposed ICA rider, considers such costs in isolation.

218          Piecemeal ratemaking also ignores the dynamics of ratemaking.  After rates are

219           established in a rate case, specific costs may increase while other costs may decrease,

220            offsetting any need  to  increase  rates  overall.   This  effectively  converts  the

221          “opportunity” to recover these certain costs into a “guarantee.” As a consequence,

222          piecemeal ratemaking: (1) shifts risks to ratepayers that are properly borne by the utility

223           shareholders; (2) fails to balance ratepayer and shareholder interests; and (3) will not

224          provide RMP the proper incentive to minimize costs.


225   Q.   DOES CONVERTING THE OPPORTUNITY TO RECOVER THE COST OF

226       PROVIDING SERVICE INTO A GUARANTEE REDUCE THE COMPANY’S

227       OPERATING AND FINANCIAL RISKS?

228   A.     Yes. Given this, I recommend the Commission reject the proposed ICA rider or, if

229          approved, reduce RMP’s ROE accordingly, in addition to the ROE recommendations

230        made by UTLCG witness, Mr. David Garrett, in Phase I.





           Direct Testimony of                                        Docket No. 24-035-04
            Billie S. LaConte                                     Utah Large Customer Group

                                         Page 13231   Q.  WHY ARE YOU CONCERNED WITH THE LACK OF DEFINITION

232       ASSOCIATED WITH COSTS TRACKABLE IN THE ICA RIDER?

233   A.   A tracker should have a defined scope of allowable costs, similar to the Energy

234          Balancing Account (“EBA”).  Without such limits, RMP would be incentivized to

235           include in the ICA rider as many categories of costs as possible (given the effectively

236          guaranteed recovery). Following RMP’s inclusion of costs, dispute is likely to arise

237           regarding whether, and the extent to which, costs are appropriately included in the rider

238           or should be included in base rates. Further, the Company is proposing that any excess

239        ICA collections be used to fund a self-insurance reserve.15  This proposal deprives

240          customers the ability to use the excess dollars paid into the ICA, even if RMP doesn’t

241          need the funds at this time or even anytime soon. Thus, having a defined set of costs

242           appropriate for rider recovery is critical before approving rider recovery. This allows

243           the Commission and stakeholders to evaluate the need for, and magnitude of, potential

244            rider recovery. Therefore, the Commission should reject the ICA rider at this time.

247   A.     Yes. As I discuss below, RMP’s proposed allocation of ICA costs is not consistent

248          with cost causation.  If the ICA rider is approved despite its flaws, the Commission

249          should require ICA costs to be allocated similar to the Wildfire Mitigation Balancing

250          Account.

260       OPERATIONS. DO YOU AGREE?

263           are impacted by the operation and maintenance (“O&M”) of the T&D systems. ELI

264          has little or nothing to do with production plant, non-fuel O&M expense, or EBA-

265            related expenses. Therefore, it would be inappropriate to allocate the ICA rider costs

266         on base revenues that reflect these unrelated expenses.

286   Q.   PLEASE SUMMARIZE YOUR RECOMMENDATIONS RELATED TO THE

Response to OCS Data Request 10.3

        (a) Yes. The Company intends to include the punitive damages insurance policy
           cost in its proposed Insurance Cost Adjustment.

        (b) The insurability of punitive damages varies by state.

         The punitive damage coverage is not in addition to or additional limits. The
            limit is shared, and the wrap is put in place only to address the gap in
          coverage where a United States (U.S.) policy is not able to respond. The
           punitive damage policy is intended to wrap around the U.S. policy terms and
          only indemnify the insured for punitive damage losses. All punitive damage
         wrap policies are self-procured. Other key exclusions to coverage of note:

          •  Excludes any punitive damages agreed to in a settlement.
          •  Excludes coverage for any punitive damages, which compulsory damages
               are not covered.
          •  Excludes defense for punitive damages.

           Punitive damage wrap policies are intended to “wrap” around a traditional
           insurance policy to provide coverage for punitive damages. The policies do
          not provide any additional limit – the limit is shared with the U.S. issued
           policy that is being wrapped. Puni-wraps are placed out of Bermuda and are
           not subject to U.S. laws.                                                                        Exhibit BSL-324-035-04 / Rocky Mountain Power                                                            Page 2 of 2
