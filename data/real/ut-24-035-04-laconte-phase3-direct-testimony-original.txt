 88   Q.  WHAT IS EXCESS LIABILITY INSURANCE?

 89   A.    ELI provides coverage for liability to third parties arising out of bodily injury and

 90           property damage losses suffered by those third parties. It includes coverage for general

 91             liability, wildfire liability, and employer’s liability. Claims for damages to third parties

 92           are included within excess liability coverage.


 93   Q.  HOW DOES RMP ACQUIRE ITS ELI COVERAGE?

 94   A.     PacifiCorp d/b/a RMP, through  its parent company, Berkshire Hathaway Energy

 95        Company (“BHE”), purchases ELI as part of BHE’s aggregated insurance purchase.

 96         The ELI premiums are allocated to PacifiCorp and other BHE subsidiaries through

 97        BHE’s corporate allocation, which is based on an average percentage of: property,

 98            plant, and equipment; employee count; loss history; overhead electric T&D lines; and

 99      T&D pipeline miles.3

103           Utah-allocated basis using the System Overhead allocation factor from the 2020 Multi-

104           State Cost Allocation Protocol.

                                         Page 11                                    III. INSURANCE COST ADJUSTMENT


193   Q.  WHY IS RMP PROPOSING TO IMPLEMENT THE ICA RIDER?

194   A.   RMP states that the proposed ICA rider would allow the Company to track and recover

195         ELI costs and collections, separate and apart from base rates.  Additionally, the

196        Company plans to use this mechanism to recover other as-yet undefined insurance

197            related costs, and potentially combine commercial insurance and Company self-

198           insurance in this rider.12


199   Q.   ARE THERE ANY PROBLEMS WITH THE PROPOSED ICA RIDER?

200   A.     Yes.  First, the proposed ICA rider constitutes single-issue or piecemeal ratemaking.

201          Second, it is unclear whether the proposed ICA rider would only recover insurance

202          premiums. According to RMP’s responses to data requests, the Company would also

203           include punitive damage insurance policy costs in the proposed ICA rider.13 However,

204           the proposed rider does not have a list or any description of the specific costs that would

205          be eligible for recovery. Third, RMP’s proposal to spread the costs across classes using

206          base revenues is not consistent with cost-causation principles.


207   Q.  WHAT  DO  YOU  MEAN  BY  SINGLE-ISSUE  OR  PIECEMEAL

208       RATEMAKING?

209   A.    Piecemeal ratemaking occurs whenever a utility is allowed to implement a tracker that



          12 Amended Direct Testimony of Joelle R. Steward, p. 6, lines 114-120.
          13 RMP Response to OCS Data Request 10.3, a copy of which is included in Exhibit BSL-3.

           Direct Testimony of                                        Docket No. 24-035-04
            Billie S. LaConte                                     Utah Large Customer Group

                                         Page 12210           guarantees dollar-for-dollar cost recovery.  Piecemeal ratemaking allows a utility to

211           adjust rates outside of, and absent the scrutiny provided in, a general rate case. Such

212           guarantees are contrary to the regulatory compact in which a utility should be allowed

213          a reasonable opportunity to earn a reasonable rate of return on the facilities used to

214          provide electricity service and to recover reasonable and necessary operating expenses.

215        When costs are evaluated in a general rate case, they are considered comprehensively,

216          whereas a tracker, such as the proposed ICA rider, considers such costs in isolation.

217          Piecemeal ratemaking also ignores the dynamics of ratemaking.  After rates are

218           established in a rate case, specific costs may increase while other costs may decrease,

219            offsetting any need  to  increase  rates  overall.   This  effectively  converts  the

220          “opportunity” to recover these certain costs into a “guarantee.” As a consequence,

221          piecemeal ratemaking: (1) shifts risks to ratepayers that are properly borne by the utility

222           shareholders; (2) fails to balance ratepayer and shareholder interests; and (3) will not

223          provide RMP the proper incentive to minimize costs.


224   Q.   DOES CONVERTING THE OPPORTUNITY TO RECOVER THE COST OF

225       PROVIDING SERVICE INTO A GUARANTEE REDUCE THE COMPANY’S

226       OPERATING AND FINANCIAL RISKS?

227   A.     Yes. Given this, I recommend the Commission reject the proposed ICA rider or, if

228          approved, reduce RMP’s ROE accordingly, in addition to the ROE recommendations

229        made by UTLCG witness, Mr. David Garrett, in Phase I.

            Billie S. LaConte                                     Utah Large Customer Group

                                         Page 13230   Q.  WHY ARE YOU CONCERNED WITH THE LACK OF DEFINITION

231       ASSOCIATED WITH COSTS TRACKABLE IN THE ICA RIDER?

232   A.   A tracker should have a defined scope of allowable costs, similar to the Energy

233          Balancing Account (“EBA”).  Without such limits, RMP would be incentivized to

234           include in the ICA rider as many categories of costs as possible (given the effectively

235          guaranteed recovery). Following RMP’s inclusion of costs, dispute is likely to arise

236           regarding whether, and the extent to which, costs are appropriately included in the rider

237           or should be included in base rates. Further, the Company is proposing that any excess

238        ICA collections be used to fund a self-insurance reserve.14  This proposal deprives

239          customers the ability to use the excess dollars paid into the ICA, even if RMP doesn’t

240          need the funds at this time or even anytime soon. Thus, having a defined set of costs

241           appropriate for rider recovery is critical before approving rider recovery. This allows

242           the Commission and stakeholders to evaluate the need for, and magnitude of, potential

243            rider recovery. Therefore, the Commission should reject the ICA rider at this time.


244   Q.    IF THE COMMISSION APPROVES THE ICA RIDER, DO YOU HAVE ANY

245      RECOMMENDATIONS?

246   A.     Yes. As I discuss below, RMP’s proposed allocation of ICA costs is not consistent

247          with cost causation.  If the ICA rider is approved despite its flaws, the Commission

248          should require ICA costs to be allocated similar to the Wildfire Mitigation Balancing

249          Account.

263          has little or nothing to do with production plant, non-fuel O&M expense, or EBA-

264            related expenses. Therefore, it would be inappropriate to allocate the ICA rider costs

265         on base revenues that reflect these unrelated expenses.
