5. Grandfathering:
a. The High Load Customer Terms and Conditions, including the new minimum demand 
obligations in the various rate schedules, shall not apply to the load of any customer 
that is governed by an ESA with Dominion, or began service under other arrangements, 
before January 1, 2026, unless the customer elects otherwise.
b. Membership in the GS-5 rate class shall be mandatory for customers subject to the High 
Load Customer Terms and Conditions regarding load for which service begun on or 
after January 1, 2026. Customers that executed an ESA or began service under other 
arrangements before January 1, 2026 shall not be moved from their existing current 
rate class but may opt in to the High Load Customer Terms and Conditions and GS-5 
rate schedule at any time.
7. Capacity Reductions: Customers subject to the High Load Customer Terms and Conditions 
shall be permitted to:
a. Before January 1, 2027, adjust their MWs downward without penalty.
b. After January 1,2027, reduce their capacity one time by up to 20% without penalty and 
after 36-months’ notice to Dominion; and
c. At anytime, reduce their capacity up to 100% of their contract capacity, as follows:
i. Must provide 36-months notice to Dominion, but with flexibility to relinquish 
capacity sooner;
ii. Without any fee, provided that Dominion can re-market the capacity within a 
reasonable time period. Dominion shall make reasonable efforts to re-market 
all capacity made available as a result of a customer’s reduction.
iii. Upon paying a capacity reduction fee if the capacity cannot be re-marketed 
within a reasonable time period. The capacity reduction fee shall not exceed 
five years of minimum demand charges.
d. The High Load customer may work with Dominion to facilitate the reassignment to 
another customer.

8. Capacity Reporting: Dominion shall track the total amount of capacity reductions requested 
and implemented following the Commission’s final decision in this biennial review proceeding 
and submit reports to Staff annually. The information in the report shall be aggregated, 
submitted as confidential, and made available upon request to respondents in future IRP and 
biennial proceeding pursuant to appropriate protective agreements.
9. Exit  Fees:
a. Notwithstanding the Contract Term described in para. 10, a customer may elect to 
terminate its contract at any time, subject to a three-year notice requirement and 
payment of an exit fee. The amount of the exit fee shall not exceed five years of 
minimum demand charges. For example, if a customer provided notice at the 
conclusion of Year 2, the customer would be subject to minimum demand charges for 
years 3 through 5, and an exit fee at the conclusion of year 5 that shall not exceed five 
years of minimum demand charges. Dominion shall make reasonable efforts to mitigate 
any exit fee by securing offsetting revenues or otherwise mitigating costs.

12. Allocation Factor for generation: The Average and Excess allocation method shall be used 
for allocation of fixed generation plant costs for this Biennial Review. Nothing in this Proposal 
seeks to prohibit any party or Staff in a future biennial review proceeding from proposing an 
alternative allocation method for generation plant costs.
13. High Load Factor Queue: The parties agree to convene a working group to review the High 
Load Factor customer queue process and batch study process delays within 7 business days of 
the issuance of the final order in this biennial review proceeding. The workgroup will submit 
a report in Case No. PUR-2024-00144 within 90 days of issuance of the final order in this case. 
The report will include an aggregated summary of project delays, review of the effectiveness 
of the queue, and opportunities to prioritize interconnection of large load projects that include 
energy efficiency, demand response, advanced transmission technologies, and other potential 
investments that would increase capacity and provide system-wide cost-savings and reliability 
benefits to all customers on the system. The report shall include a description and history of 
the queue, and consensus and non-consensus issues to improve the process to bring more 
projects online faster. The submission of the report shall be followed by a hearing, if deemed 
necessary by the Commission, concluded within nine months of the final order in this 
proceeding, to resolve issues associated with the queue process, including any changes to 
ensure the transparency and accuracy of Dominion’s load forecasting methodology.
