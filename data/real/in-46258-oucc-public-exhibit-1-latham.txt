V. 
THE FIVE PILLARS 
Q:  
What are the “Five Pillars of Electric Utility Service” in Indiana? 
2 
A: 
The Five Pillars were identified by the Indiana 21st Century Energy Policy 
3 
Development Task Force and codified in Ind. Code § 8-1-2-0.6. The Five Pillars 
4 
are reliability, affordability, resiliency, stability, and environmental sustainability. 
5 
Q: 
How does Indiana state policy on the “Five Pillars” apply to this request? 
6 
A: 
The Indiana General Assembly enacted two separate policy statements regarding 
7 
utility service in Indiana. The first policy was passed in 2016 recognizing 
8 
affordability and encouraged investment in infrastructure “while protecting the 
9 
affordability of utility services for present and future generations of Indiana 
10 
citizens.”4 This was codified in Ind. Code § 8-1-2-0.5. Amidst many rate increases, 
11 
the Indiana General Assembly passed an additional policy statement in 2023. Ind. 
12 
Code § 8-1-2-0.6 requires decisions concerning Indiana’s electric generation 
13 
resource mix, energy infrastructure, and electric service ratemaking constructs 
14 
consider reliability, affordability, resiliency, stability, and environmental 
15 
sustainability, referred to as the “Five Pillars of Electric Utility Service.”  
16

Capital Structure  
Q:  
Why is it important for the Commission to consider capital structure and 
1 
equity infusions when determining an authorized ROE? 
2 
A:  
The Company’s decision to increase the equity portion of its capital structure 
3 
means customers will already be paying more for the same level of service, even 
4 
before any change in ROE is applied. Because equity is more expensive than debt, 
5 
the Company’s choice to shift its capital structure toward equity drives up its 
6 
overall cost of capital. If the Commission were to approve AESI’s proposed ROE 
7 
on top of this higher equity ratio, customers would bear a double increase in 
8 
financing costs — first from the equity increase itself, and then from the higher 
9 
return applied to that larger equity base. For this reason, AESI’s decision to 
10 
increase its equity ratio is a critical factor in setting a fair ROE that balances the 
11 
Company’s financial health with customer affordability. 
12
