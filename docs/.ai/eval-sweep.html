The four constants, measured

ADR-85 records four rules in app/state/mapping.py as judgements: a four-character floor on a term, a prefix test rather than a substring test, discarding a word more than half the company's duties share, and a two-word minimum before a duty is offered. Each was tuned by eye against fixtures. Nobody had swept any of them. This is the sweep, run 2026-08-05.

One command reproduces every number on this page.
.venv/bin/python scripts/sweep_mapping_constants.py
It points STRATA_DATABASE_URL at a scratch file before importing app.state.db, exactly as tests/conftest.py does, so it cannot touch a working database. It changes no source file: it sets the four constants on the live module and calls the real propose_obligations_for_change.
The gold set in data/evals/ could not be used, and that is the first honest thing on this page. Both files there label a different task. goldset_ga_ky.json and goldset_ut_mo.json ask, of a passage of a real filing, whether it creates an obligation; app/evals/obligations.py scores that, and its Extraction carries asserted, obligation_text and who_is_bound. Neither file holds a single change-to-obligation label, and mapping.py answers a different question: given a change the diff found, which of THIS COMPANY'S recorded duties does it bear on. There is no way to score the second with the first, so this page does not pretend to. What it borrows from that scorer is its vocabulary and its refusal to average two costs into one number.

What the labels are, and who wrote them

The synthetic corpus carries two independent statements about which change bears on which duty. Both were written by the corpus author before this module existed. Both are used, because they disagree in one place and the disagreement is exactly where the sweep's result turns.

Label setWhere it comes fromCoverage
MANIFESTdata/manifest.json names five changes and, for each, the obligation it bears on. CHG-5 names a second. Projected onto the diff rows whose offsets fall inside those five regions.10 of the 27 diff rows. 14 true pairs, 66 false pairs.
SECTIONdata/company_context.json gives every obligation a maps_to_docket_sections list. Matched against each diff row's own section label for the row's to-version.All 27 diff rows. 11 true pairs, 205 false pairs.

They disagree about the restructure. The manifest labels the whole moved block — Sections 5.4, 5.4.1, 5.4.2 and 5.4.3 in v3 — as bearing on OBL-001 and OBL-008 together, because that is the granularity a change label has. The obligation register is finer: OBL-001 lists only 5.4.1, and 5.4.3 belongs to OBL-004 and OBL-008. So the pairs CHG-v2-v3-006 / OBL-001 and CHG-v2-v3-007 / OBL-001 are correct offers under MANIFEST and wrong offers under SECTION. Every result below where the two label sets differ traces to those two pairs. Neither reading is obviously wrong: 5.4.2 is the posting deadline for the collateral OBL-001 is about.

What the columns mean

ColumnMeaning
wrongPairs where the proposer cleared its threshold and the labels say the change does not bear on that duty. The scorer in app/evals/obligations.py calls the analogous thing WRONG_ASSERTION. The word is borrowed and not earned: this module proposes and never asserts, and the screen renders a candidate differently from a confirmed mapping. It is still the count an analyst pays for, one row at a time.
missedPairs the labels hold and the proposer refused. A duty a person has to map by hand.
rightOffered, and the labels agree.
refusalsEvery pair the proposer declined, right ones and wrong ones together — missed plus the correct refusals. It is the accounting the module already prints on every screen.
offers/changeThe reading load: how many duties an analyst is asked about on the average change, and on the worst one.

The measure is reason == "", which is the proposer's own test for "these words cleared the threshold", rather than ObligationMatch.offered. offered also folds in whether somebody already mapped or rejected the pair, and that is a fact about the seed, not about the rule under test.

There is a hole at four and five. The shipped rule cuts into that hole, which is why three of its settings score identically, and why the whole rule on this corpus removes exactly one word.

Not defensible as the best value, and the measurement says so cleanly. Under SECTION labels, five strictly dominates four: 29 fewer wrong offers, the same three misses, the same eight correct offers. Nothing is traded. Under MANIFEST labels five removes 16 wrong offers and costs two correct ones — and the two are CHG-v2-v3-006 / OBL-001 and CHG-v2-v3-007 / OBL-001, the pairs the two label sets already disagree about. So the gain is not arguable and the cost is.

Why five is different. At four the surviving short words are load, post, work, file, rate, that, with, each. The list of wrong offers at the shipped setting is nearly all of them: OBL-007 is offered against fourteen of the twenty-seven changes, every one of them on customer and load; OBL-006 comes up on interconnection, with, each and OBL-005 on customer, request, that, rate. The module docstring already names this failure — "with, each, that and before are four characters long and appear in nearly every filing" — and treats it as unfixable without document-frequency weighting. It is partly fixable by moving the floor one character.

The constant is imported from app/state/search.py, and it is not doing the same job in the two places. In search.py, PREFIX_MIN is how many letters a term must END with before the query ALSO searches it as a prefix. It widens a search that already matched exactly, and search.py measured it that way: "curtailment" as an exact token found 9 of 15 passages, and widening recovered the six that said "Curtailments". In mapping.py the same number is a FLOOR: a word shorter than it is thrown away and never compared at all. Widening a match and discarding evidence are different acts, and the argument measured for the first does not carry to the second. The alignment mapping.py defends in its docstring — a ligature or a soft hyphen reading the same here as in the index — is carried by index_body(), which is imported separately. The module also states that nothing in it queries the index. So a separate floor here would not break the folding, and this file is where that should be argued.

At a one-word threshold, substring adds nine wrong offers on SECTION labels. Eight of the nine are OBL-008 pulled in by connection sitting inside interconnection, which reaches almost every change in the docket. That is the same shape as the "work" in "network" case and it is larger.

Defensible, and free at the shipped settings, but the docstring overstates it. The prefix rule costs nothing anywhere in this sweep — it never loses a correct offer at a two-word threshold — and it removes a wrong offer, so keep it. What it does not do is what the docstring implies: "every passage about network upgrades proposed the security-posting duty" describes the world before MIN_MATCHED_TERMS existed. Today the two-word minimum absorbs almost all of it. At a three-word threshold the substring rule finds MORE correct pairs than the prefix rule on SECTION labels (8 against 6) for two extra wrong offers, so "substring is wrong here" is true at the shipped point and not as a general statement.

Three. A word more than half the duties share is not evidence

Swept as "drop a word held by more than N of the eight duties". The shipped rule is "more than half", which on eight duties is N = 4. N = 8 turns the rule off.

The rule earns its keep. The threshold is unmeasurable at its own boundary, and it sits on the wrong side of the only gap in the distribution. Three findings, in order of how uncomfortable they are.
  1. N = 3, 4 and 5 are identical. No word is held by exactly four or five of the eight duties, so "more than half" cannot be distinguished from "more than three eighths" or "more than five eighths" on this corpus. The chosen value is not tuned to noise. It is also not measured: the sweep cannot see it.
  2. The whole rule removes one word. Turning it off (N = 8) restores large and nothing else, because large is the only term above the cut. Removing that one word is worth 21 fewer wrong offers on SECTION labels for one fewer correct one. The docstring's own account — six of eight duties carry "large", and before this rule every change about a Large Load Customer proposed six duties at once — is confirmed, and it is the entire effect.
  3. The four words just under the cut do most of the remaining damage. customer, interconnection, load and file are each held by three of eight. Dropping them too (N = 2) removes another 40 wrong offers on SECTION labels with no change to the misses and no change to the correct offers. Under MANIFEST it costs the same two disputed OBL-001 pairs and nothing else.

The rule is stated as a fraction, so tightening it from "more than half" to "more than a quarter" is a claim about companies generally, not about these eight duties. Eight is too few to make that claim. A company with eighty duties would have a word held by three of them mean something quite different, and this sweep says nothing about it.

Defensible, and the reason given for it is not the reason the numbers support. The docstring says one shared word is a coincidence and the second is what makes a candidate worth a glance. The step from one to two is the one step in this whole sweep that buys almost no accuracy: 20 per cent of offers are right at one word and 21 per cent at two under MANIFEST labels, 11 per cent and 12 per cent under SECTION. What the step does buy is reading load. It cuts the average change from 3.8 duties to 2.4 and the worst change from seven to six, for three misses. That is a real and worthwhile purchase, and it is a claim about an analyst's afternoon rather than about coincidence. Every step above two does raise accuracy, and steadily: there is no knee in the curve and nothing distinguishes two as a value except that it is where the reading load starts to fall.

A defect the sweep found

MATCH_ONE_WORD is hard-wired to MIN_MATCHED_TERMS == 2, and nothing enforces it. The reason code is chosen by MATCH_ONE_WORD if best_terms else MATCH_NO_OVERLAP. Any near miss with at least one word gets the code whose note reads "One word in common, which is under the threshold". That is true only while the threshold is two. Raise the constant and the note starts lying on a screen, silently, to the person the note exists for:

The fix is small — either name the code for what it means (under the threshold, with the count in the note) or add a guard that refuses a threshold the codes cannot describe.

What this does NOT show, said before anybody asks

  • Nothing here is held out. Every setting was scored on the same 27 changes. Eleven true pairs under SECTION labels, fourteen under MANIFEST. A recommendation drawn from a sweep over eleven positives is a fit, not a validation, and moving a shipped constant on it would be tuning to a corpus of eight duties.
  • Both label sets came from the same hand as the corpus. They say whether the rule finds what the corpus author intended, not whether it finds what a regulatory analyst would call a match. The gold sets in data/evals/ are the ones labelled without reading the code, and they are for the other task.
  • Eight duties is a small denominator for a rule stated as a fraction. The shared-word rule counts holders against a total of eight. Its behaviour at eighty is not measured and cannot be inferred from this.
  • The 27 changes come from one synthetic docket. The real corpus in data/real/ has no obligation register attached to it, so this could not run there.
  • Nothing here measures the ranking or MAX_CANDIDATES. The sweep reads reason == "", which is the threshold and not the shortlist.

What to do

Do not move the constants on this evidence. The sweep found that two of the four are not at their measured optimum, and it found it on eleven positive pairs from a corpus written alongside the product. Changing a shipped rule on that would be the mistake this repository keeps writing down: a number that looks measured and is fitted. The right output is this page.

What would earn a change, in order of cost:

  1. Label the change-to-obligation task the way data/evals/ labels extraction. A file of change-and-duty pairs, labelled by somebody who has not read mapping.py, over more than one docket. Then app/evals/obligations.py gets a sibling and the shipped values are answerable.
  2. Split PREFIX_MIN. The import is defended on an alignment that index_body() carries by itself, and the module states that nothing in it queries the index. A floor of its own, argued in this file, would let retrieval and mapping each hold the number their own measurement supports.
  3. Put a guard between MIN_MATCHED_TERMS and MATCH_ONE_WORD so the note on the screen cannot go stale when the constant moves. That one is worth doing whatever happens to the rest.
Where the code is. scripts/sweep_mapping_constants.py. It carries the same account of the label sets and their limits in its module docstring, so a reader who runs it without finding this page still gets told what the numbers do not cover. It is not wired into the Makefile and it is not part of make test: it takes about a minute, it seeds a scratch database, and it measures rather than asserts.
