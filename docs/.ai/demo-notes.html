What AI Fund Demoed

Primary source: notes taken during Rishabh Singhal's demo at the AI Builder Lunch with Andrew Ng, Mountain View, 2026-08-03. Julia Lundin (VP AES Next) and Federico Heine (AES, LatAm innovation) in the room.

Why this file exists. This is the closest thing we have to AI Fund's own picture of Strata, described by the person leading the build. The submission should be visibly aware of it — either building toward it, or deliberately scoping narrower and saying why. A submission that reinvents a product its authors already described, without acknowledging the description, reads as not having listened.

The screens, as described

A project list. Each project carries a running list of regulatory changes attached to it.

Per project: research threads, work plans, scheduled re-runs. Knowledge and intelligence keeps building on itself.

A review centre: sources (internal and external), analytics, expert review, all findings, questions, the collective take, deliverables, and "steer" directives to shift the research.

The themes stated alongside them

  • Trust and human review — getting people comfortable with AI is the adoption problem, not a feature.
  • Make it easy for a human to inject knowledge.
  • Keep it up to date proactively.

Also from the event listing: the platform converts "enterprise work from static research and one-off documents into living, goal-oriented projects", and the co-founder profile sought is "technologists with demonstrated leadership at an early-stage B2B SaaS company, ideally from 0 to 1 and into early scale".

What we are building against it

The 48-hour build implements a slice, deliberately. Naming which slice, and why, is a stronger panel answer than a thin version of everything.

What was demoedIn our build?Reasoning
Project list with a running list of changes per projectYesThis is the spine. Our change-to-action wedge produces exactly this, and the company context carries projects for changes to attach to.
Review centre: findings, expert review, escalationYes, the trust core of itThe escalation queue and audit trail are the parts that make the rest safe. We build those and skip analytics and the "collective take" — they present conclusions, and presentation without verified provenance is the failure this product exists to prevent.
Sources, internal and externalPartlyInternal company context and external proceeding versions, both ingested. Live source polling is roadmap, not build.
Work plansPartlyWe produce recommended actions with reviewer routing, which is the atom of a work plan. Plan-level sequencing and dependencies are out.
Research threadsNoA research thread is an open-ended investigation. Our unit is a change with a deterministic diff behind it. Adding research would blur the one thing that makes our claims verifiable.
Scheduled re-runsNo, but designed forRe-running is what keeps state current, and it is the exact pattern where staleness hides (a derived value nobody recomputes looks identical to a fresh one). The audit and version model supports it; the scheduler is roadmap.
"Steer" directives to shift researchNoFollows research threads. Worth noting that a durable steer has to be auditable, or it is a one-turn illusion.
Knowledge compounding on itselfNamed as the moat, not builtThe MRD argues this is the durable advantage: the mapping between a regulator's wording and a company's own. It compounds per customer and cannot be bought. It also does not exist at launch, which we say plainly.
AnalyticsNoAn analytics screen for a regulatory analyst is a feature engineers want. The prep guide names exactly this trap. If asked, the honest answer is that it serves no pain we identified.

Where we diverge on purpose

Two places, both worth being able to defend out loud.

1. We put citation verification underneath everything, and the demo did not emphasise it. The description centres on breadth — projects, threads, plans, re-runs, a review centre. Our bet is that none of it is usable in a regulated enterprise unless each claim can be traced to the exact words behind it and refuses to assert itself when it cannot. If that bet is wrong, we built the right mechanism for the wrong reason; the user interviews (H3 in user-research.html) are what would tell us.

2. We chose depth over surface. Given 48 hours, a thin version of nine screens demonstrates nothing about engineering judgement. One workflow that survives adversarial questioning demonstrates a great deal. That is a deliberate trade against a demo that would look more like theirs.

The line to have ready

If asked why the build does not look like what was demoed:

What you showed is the product. What I built is the load-bearing wall — one change, from two versions of a docket through to a routed action, where every claim carries a citation the system verifies before it will assert it. I chose that over a thinner version of the whole surface because the review centre is only worth having if what reaches it can be trusted, and trust is the part that cannot be added later.
