Rubric Briefing

What AI Fund evaluates, what they have told us they ask, and what your answer is. Source: AI Fund's own preparation guide, Preparing for the Build Challenge (updated 2026-06-05), read in full.

The one line that reframes everything: "The Build Challenge is not a coding test. It is a compressed simulation of the first two weeks of the residency." They are not scoring a deliverable. They are watching how you work, and inferring whether they want you in the room for four weeks and possibly on the founding team after. Every rule below follows from that.

Questions they have told us they ask

These are close to verbatim from the guide. Have an answer for each, in your own words, out loud, before the panel.

Technical

Why this retrieval method over that one?

Lexical first, semantic only where paraphrase matters. Regulatory text is quotation-heavy and citation-bearing, so exact terms carry meaning and lexical search is strong, debuggable and free. Company obligations are written in the company's own words and will never lexically match the docket's — that specific join is where embeddings earn their place. Concede without being asked: the corpus is far too small to demonstrate a retrieval difference, so this is accepted on reasoning, not measurement (ADR-008).

What happens when retrieval fails on some kind of query?

This is the strongest structural answer in the design, so do not rush it. Retrieval does not enumerate changes — the deterministic diff does. Retrieval only ranks context for interpretation. So a retrieval miss degrades explanation quality, it does not lose a change. That separation was the point of ADR-004. Then distinguish a retrieval miss from a retrieval service failure: a dead provider or corrupt index escalates to review rather than silently degrading, because a degraded impact mapping that announces nothing is the failure mode this whole design exists to prevent.

What is your fallback when the model is wrong?

The design assumes the model will be wrong and makes that survivable. A misquotation is caught by offset verification and never reaches the user as fact. A misjudged materiality is caught by confidence thresholds and escalates. What is not caught: a fluent, correctly-cited, wrong interpretation of a passage the model quoted accurately. Say that plainly — it is the honest limit, and naming it is worth more than pretending the verifier covers it.

How would you handle a 10x increase in latency?

The user-facing path is not the model path. Ingestion, diffing and verification are deterministic and fast; the model runs on interpretation, which is asynchronous work producing review items rather than a blocking request. So a 10x model latency lengthens the queue, it does not freeze the workspace. At that point the levers are batching interpretations per change set, caching by content hash so an unchanged passage is never re-interpreted, and degrading to diff-only output with everything escalated.

Where does modern AI fail here, and how did you design around it?

Three, and they are documented rather than improvised. Fluent misquotation — models produce plausible, subtly wrong quotes and prompting does not fix it; answered by verifying against source offsets. Silent degradation — a provider falls back or a signal becomes meaningless and everything downstream still returns confident output; answered by refusing to score a signal that cannot be computed. False precision — a 0.87 confidence implies calibration nobody has done; answered with coarse ordinal buckets, calibrated against eval outcomes rather than taken on faith.

Attack the citation verifier. Where does exact-offset matching break?

Lead with the one they may not find: wrong-occurrence citations. Regulatory documents repeat boilerplate, so a citation can carry exact offsets landing on occurrence A while the model reasoned about occurrence B. The verifier confirms the quote matches at those offsets and passes it — green, and wrong. The corpus contains that trap deliberately (nine occurrences of one sentence) so it can be demonstrated and fixed rather than merely described. Then the rest: tables lose row structure when flattened and a cell offset is not gradable without its header; footnotes have no anchor to the clause they qualify; PDF extraction introduces ligatures and column bleed that the synthetic corpus does not contain, so the golden-file tests do not cover the risk they appear to.

Product

Who is this for, specifically?

One sentence, no hedging: a regulatory affairs analyst at a multi-state investor-owned utility. Then their day — how change reaches them, what they do with two versions of a docket, who they must convince, and where the record of that work currently lives.

Why are alerts and search insufficient?

An alert says something moved; the analyst still opens both versions, finds the delta, judges materiality, decides whether it binds yet, and works out which of their own obligations it touches. Search is worse — it assumes they already know what to look for. The expensive part is interpretation, and neither tool does any of it.

What did users tell you, and what changed because of it?

The one you cannot fake. Whatever happens, answer with specifics: what they misread, what they ignored, what they asked for that was not there. If only one conversation lands, say so and report it honestly — one real conversation reported accurately beats three implied ones, and they can spot an unfilled template instantly.

What does this become in 12 weeks, and in six months?

Explicitly asked for. 12 weeks: live docket ingestion instead of uploaded files, and a second jurisdiction proving the taxonomy generalises. Six months: the obligation graph as the compounding asset — the mapping between how a regulator words a requirement and how a specific company words its own, which is per-customer, cannot be bought, and is the actual moat. The verifier and the diff are the floor, not the moat; say that before they say it to you.

AI fluency and founder-market fit

What did you use AI to build, and what did you decide yourself?

The defensible shape is "I chose this approach because X, and asked the AI to implement it." The disqualifying shape is "the AI picked it and I shipped it" — and the guide says the panel finds out quickly. Keep docs/submission.html current as you build; the rejections are the most valuable rows in it, because rejecting a proposal is evidence of judgement in a way accepting one is not.

Why are you drawn to this problem?

Founder-market fit is a named thread. Your honest answer is not domain love — you are not an energy person, and claiming otherwise with AES in the room would be a mistake. It is that this is a trust-engineering problem in a domain where being confidently wrong is expensive, and you have spent the last year building systems whose hardest lessons were exactly that. Say the true thing.

Concede these before you are asked

Volunteering a limit converts it from a hole they found into discipline you showed. Each of these is true, and each will be found.

  • "Verified" means the quote is real, not that the interpretation is right. Offset verification proves the words exist where claimed. It does not prove the model reasoned about them correctly.
  • The eval set is small and self-labelled. It is a sanity check, not an accuracy claim. Do not defend the number.
  • Retrieval is architecturally present, not demonstrated. The corpus is too small to show a difference.
  • Ordinal confidence is not inherently more trustworthy than a decimal. It is a coarser bucket that resists false precision, and it needs calibrating against eval outcomes.
  • The moat does not exist at launch. The verifier and diff are table stakes; the obligation graph has to be earned customer by customer.
  • Cold start is a real weakness of a change-centric product. One version of a proceeding produces nothing yet.

The panel itself

A conversation with AI Fund partners, usually onsite, moving through three threads: your TDD, your PRD, and AI fluency plus founder-market fit. Clear communication is stated as part of the evaluation — speak slowly, and ask a panelist to rephrase rather than guessing at the question.

Role fit, stated in the guide: "Engineer in Residence is an engineering role. If your primary background is product or business rather than engineering, talk to your recruiter before submitting." Your background is genuinely both, and your recent work is hands-on code. Lead with the engineering — the 200,000 lines shipped this year, the systems you debugged this week — and let the operating history be the second thing they learn, not the first.
