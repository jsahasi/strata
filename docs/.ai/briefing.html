Questions they have told us they ask

These are close to verbatim from the guide. Have an answer for each, in your own words, out loud, before the panel.

Technical

Why this retrieval method over that one?

Lexical first, semantic only where paraphrase matters. Regulatory text is quotation-heavy and citation-bearing, so exact terms carry meaning and lexical search is strong, debuggable and free. Company obligations are written in the company's own words and will never lexically match the docket's — that specific join is where embeddings earn their place. Concede without being asked: none of it is built. There is no index, no ranking and no embeddings anywhere in app/, and ADR-08 says so in its own entry rather than leaving the diagram to imply otherwise. This is a decision accepted on reasoning, not on measurement, and it has not been wrong yet because it has not been run.

What happens when retrieval fails on some kind of query?

The strongest structural answer in the design, so do not rush it. Retrieval does not enumerate changes — the deterministic diff does. Retrieval only ranks context for interpretation. So a retrieval miss degrades explanation quality; it does not lose a change. That separation was the point of ADR-04. Then distinguish a retrieval miss from a retrieval service failure: a dead provider or a corrupt index escalates to review rather than degrading in silence, because a degraded impact mapping that announces nothing is the failure this whole design exists to prevent.

What is your fallback when the model is wrong?

The design assumes the model will be wrong and makes that survivable, and the gate does not care what produced the claim. A misquotation is caught by offset verification and never reaches the user as fact. A misjudged materiality is caught by confidence thresholds and escalates. Nothing is repaired on the way through: a proposal whose offsets sit two characters off the quote is withheld, not nudged into place, because snapping an offset to the nearest match would make the verifier a formality checking its own repair (ADR-37). What is not caught: a fluent, correctly-cited, wrong reading of a passage the model quoted accurately. Say that plainly — naming it is worth more than pretending the verifier covers it.

How would you handle a 10x increase in latency?

The user-facing path is not the model path. Ingestion, diffing and verification are deterministic and fast — 144 changes over a million characters in 0.78 seconds on the Kentucky pair, which is the number to quote. The model runs on interpretation, which is asynchronous work producing review items rather than a blocking request. So a 10x model latency lengthens the queue; it does not freeze the workspace. The levers then are batching interpretations per change set, caching by content hash so an unchanged passage is never re-interpreted, and degrading to diff-only output with everything escalated.

Where does modern AI fail here, and how did you design around it?

Three, documented rather than improvised. Fluent misquotation — models produce plausible, subtly wrong quotes and prompting does not fix it; answered by verifying against source offsets. Silent degradation — a provider falls back or a signal becomes meaningless and everything downstream still returns confident output; answered by refusing to score a signal that cannot be computed, and by three named off-states rather than one silent one. False precision — a 0.87 confidence implies calibration nobody has done; answered with coarse ordinal buckets and an eval harness that refuses to print a rate below ten independent samples. If you want the sharpest version of the second one, tell them what happened here today: the assistant shipped wired to nothing, and answered every question with a polite, honest, entirely unnecessary "the part of me that reads the record is not wired in". The fallback announced itself exactly as designed. What it announced was untrue.

Attack the citation verifier. Where does exact-offset matching break?

Lead with the one they may not find: wrong-occurrence citations. Regulatory documents repeat boilerplate, so a citation can carry exact offsets landing on occurrence A while the model reasoned about occurrence B. The verifier confirms the quote matches at those offsets and passes it — green, and wrong. The synthetic corpus contains that trap on purpose, so it is demonstrated rather than described. Then the rest, and note which have moved: tables lose row structure when flattened and a cell offset is not gradable without its header; footnotes have no anchor to the clause they qualify. PDF extraction noise is no longer hypothetical. The real corpus is stored dirty on purpose — 79 documents carry line-numbered testimony, 35 a word hyphenated across a line break, 9 a soft hyphen or a ligature — so the normalization rules meet real input at last. What remains true is narrower: the eval corpus is still three plain ASCII files, so the golden-file numbers still do not cover the extraction risk they appear to.

How do you test an AI system?

The question has no answer until the system is split into the part a model touches and the part it cannot, and that split has to be a boundary in the code rather than an intention. Here it is one module: app/interpretation/ may call a model; ingestion, normalization, the diff, the verifier and the audit chain may not, and they are tested against known answers. Then two honest limits. The proposer has never run against the real endpoint — every test drives a deterministic fake through an injected transport, so what is proven offline is the gate, not that the endpoint returns the shape asked for. And a green suite is not a safety net: 56 passing tests did not catch an unscoped tenant read because no test asked, and a fuzzer over 60,000 generated strings found two more defects in code that already looked finished at four hundred tests. That is the argument for mutation testing over a coverage number, and it is why the 99% target was cut rather than met.

Why are alerts and search insufficient?

An alert says something moved; the analyst still opens both versions, finds the delta, judges materiality, decides whether it binds yet, and works out which of their own obligations it touches. Search is worse — it assumes they already know what to look for. The expensive part is interpretation, and neither tool does any of it. The Kentucky pair is the argument in one number: 127 characters different across a million, and a person is expected to find them by reading.

The 90-second pitch, written out

They ask for this directly. Adapt the words, keep the shape: who, what, why, what is hard. Time it — 90 seconds is roughly 220 words, which is shorter than it feels. This version was rewritten at this commit and has not been said out loud.

Regulatory affairs analysts at multi-state utilities live inside a problem nobody has solved for them. A commission publishes a revised order. Somewhere in eighty pages, three things changed that matter to them and forty that do not. They open both versions side by side and read, because that is genuinely the state of the art. Then they work out which of their own obligations each change touches, decide whether it binds yet or is still a proposal, and chase down the person who has to approve doing something about it. That takes days, and the cost of missing one is a penalty or a stranded project.

Here is the size of it. A witness in a Kentucky rate case filed a million characters of testimony and then corrected it. The correction changed 127 characters. Strata finds the 144 edits between those two filings in under a second, on the commission's own copy, with a link back to it.

The hard part is not the diff. It is trust. An analyst cannot act on a machine reading of a docket unless they can see the exact words behind every claim. So every claim in Strata carries a citation into the source, and the system re-reads the original and checks that citation before it will show the claim as fact. If it does not verify, the claim is not asserted — it goes to review with the reason. That constraint is the product.

Your hardest technical choice, explained to a non-expert

Also asked for directly. Practise this until no jargon survives — no offsets, no indices, no embeddings.

Language models are fluent liars about quotations. Ask one what a document says and it will give you a sentence that sounds exactly right, is mostly right, and has a word changed in the middle that reverses the meaning. You cannot prompt that away, and in this domain it is fatal — an analyst who cannot trust the quote has to go and read the source, which is the work we claimed to remove.

So Strata is built so that nothing gets to assert a quotation on its own say-so. When the system makes a claim, it records precisely where in the original document that claim came from — which document, which version, which stretch of text. Before showing the claim, it goes back to the original and checks that the words are really there, exactly as quoted. If they are not, the claim never appears as established fact. It goes into a review queue with the reason. That check does not care whether a model or a script produced the claim, which is the point: I wrote the constraint first, so that connecting a model later cannot weaken it. It also re-checks that the document itself has not changed underneath the claim, because a fact verified in September is not a fact verified today.

The cost is that we sometimes reject a quotation that was fine, because a stray line break or a curly quote made the check fail. That is a review someone did not need to do. I chose that direction deliberately: a rejected good citation costs a minute, and an accepted bad one costs the reason anyone would use the product.

When they ask why you, and why now

Founder-market fit is a named thread and it is not about the domain.

I spent 25 years building software people rely on, took one company from nothing to the NYSE, and shipped a generative-AI product line into an installed base last year. This year I have been building AI systems hands-on again, and the lessons that stuck were all about trust: a fallback that quietly returned plausible garbage for months while every check passed, a score nobody recomputed that went stale invisibly. I hit both again this week in my own repository. That is the same failure this product exists to prevent, in a domain where it costs real money.

What you would change — have this list ready

"Defend the choices you made and acknowledge the ones you would change." Having no answer reads worse than having several. These are honest ones for this build.

WhatWhy you would change it
Send the outreach on the first night, not the second morningThe risk table named this exact failure and named the mitigation — ask on night one, offer twenty minutes — and the mitigation was written and not executed. Everything else on this list is engineering I could redo in a day. This one is the only thing on the board that time could not compensate for, and it is the quarter of the rubric that is empty.
Derive every wiring guard from the application, and extend it to the templatesTwo view modules — 2,000 lines and nine routes — were included nowhere while base.html linked to one of them from every page. The guards now derive from app.routes, which fixed that class. It did not fix the next one: a screen can be mounted and reachable by nobody, and the first-login tour can be built, tested and never included. "Is it wired" and "can a person get there" are two questions and only the first has a guard.
Run the fuzzer before believing a finished-looking fixFour correctness findings were fixed and looked done. Fuzzing the same code over 60,000 generated strings found two more defects in it — a collapsed space claiming an empty source span, and a quote match reporting a span wider than the quote, which shifted every occurrence index after it. Both were in code that already had tests. Cheap, and it should have run first rather than last.
Fetch the real corpus on day one, not on day twoThe synthetic corpus shaped the tests, the docs and the demo for a day longer than it should have, and half a dozen documents claiming "every document here is invented" went false in one commit. The real filings also produced the best number in the submission and killed a role model that was too coarse. Both of those were available on the first morning.
Measure before refusing a numberRefusing a coverage target was right about what line coverage means and wrong about what it was doing: at the time nothing had been measured, so "coverage is the wrong number" was standing in for "we do not know". Measure first, then argue about the standard.
Label the eval set with someone who does the jobSelf-labelled ground truth measures agreement with yourself. The corpus now contains three filings where the filer published their own redline, which is the first ground truth here that nobody on this side wrote — and nothing scores against it yet.
Design cold start before the change-centric modelA product whose unit is a change produces nothing from a proceeding with one version. That is a real adoption barrier and it was discovered rather than designed for.

The AI interview — mechanics worth knowing

60 minutes, voice, started within 30 minutes of submitting, with your repository open in front of you.

What happensWhat to do
You are asked to name exact files, functions and commandsThey reconcile these against the repo afterwards. Have it open. If you cannot remember a name, say so and look — that is honest and fast, and guessing a filename that does not exist is the worst possible outcome.
You are asked to produce a small piece of new logic out loudThink out loud deliberately. The reasoning is what is being scored, not the syntax. State your assumptions, name the edge case you are worried about, then write it.
AI tools are welcome on NEW questions if you narrate the useSay what you are doing and why: "I would reach for a tool here, and what I would ask it is X." Silent use on a question about your own build is the failure mode.
Questions about your own build must come from your own headThis is the reason to know the verifier, the diff engine and the escalation logic line by line. Nothing else in the preparation substitutes for it.
No additional preparation is needed, "and none helps"Take them at their word. Rest beats cramming; the interview tests recall of work you did, not knowledge you revised.

The panel itself

A conversation with AI Fund partners, usually onsite, moving through three threads: your TDD, your PRD, and AI fluency plus founder-market fit. Clear communication is stated as part of the evaluation — speak slowly, and ask a panelist to rephrase rather than guessing at the question.

Role fit, stated in the guide: "Engineer in Residence is an engineering role. If your primary background is product or business rather than engineering, talk to your recruiter before submitting." Your background is genuinely both, and your recent work is hands-on code. Lead with the engineering — the systems you debugged this week, the migration that would have taken the live site down and the reasoning that caught it — and let the operating history be the second thing they learn, not the first.
